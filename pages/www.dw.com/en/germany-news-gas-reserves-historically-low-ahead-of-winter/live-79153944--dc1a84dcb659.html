Budget battle: Long-term investment plan? Or ruinous debt trap?

German parliamentarians began debate on the country's 2027 budget on Tuesday, with Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil of the SPD defending what he called "painful compromises" as opposition parties railed against new debt.

"My vision is a Germany of the future that is sovereign and resilient in times of crisis. A strong country that is innovative and also socially just," said Klingbeil. 

Since taking office in mid-2025, Chancellor Friedrich Merz's government has ratcheted expenditures to record levels in an effort to revive a flagging economy and rearm Germany's military. 
 
The draft 2027 budget provides for spending of €555.4 billion ($645 billion), up slightly from 2026. 

Total new borrowing is set to exceed €200 billion.

The government has earmarked €118 billion for investments in AI, data centers, research and development, rail, bridges and tunnels, as well as hospitals and schools.
 
Opposition parties universally criticized the new spending, a clear departure from years of tight budgets that hewed to the sacrosanct "black zero," meaning zero debt.

Now, the Merz government is on track to rack up roughly €1 trillion in debt by the end of its term, something the Left Party's Dietmar Bartsch called "madness."

The Greens and the AfD also accused Klingbeil of using "unconstitutional accounting tricks" to get around debt rules.

Klingbeil argued the country had no other choice but to incur debt if it planned on defending itself, noting that infrastructure in the country had also been sorely under-financed over the years as well.

The budget will come up for a vote in November and many changes are expected over the next several weeks.

Protester shot in face with airgun at anti-AfD rally

A protester was shot in the face with an airgun at an anti-AfD rally in the eastern German city of Halle late Monday according to a police statement issued on Tuesday morning.  

The incident occurred at the end of a march attended by roughly 850 people and authorities say the 25-year-old was not seriously injured.

German pop singer Xavier Naidoo to be tried for incitement

Xavier Naidoo, a German pop singer, will face trial for incitement in an incident involving public antisemitic insults directed at a member of the Amadeu Antonio Foundation — which bills itself as an independent NGO dedicated to strengthening democratic civil society and combating neo-Nazism, right-wing extremism, antisemitism, racism and other forms of bigotry and hate in Germany.

On Monday, a state court in the city of Mannheim, where the musician was born, announced that it would allow state prosecutors to take their case against Naidoo to trial.

Prosecutors sought to bring charges against the celebrity in separate cases in 2023 and 2024, which the court previously rejected for lack of evidence. Prosecutors have lodged a complaint with the Higher Regional Court in Karlsruhe to force the lower court to hear their case on further points.  

Monday's decision regards one charge brought in 2023. No trial date has been set. 

A popular figure in Germany in the 2000s, Naidoo later made headlines for espousing conspiracy theories, railing against Coronavirus safety measures and hurling antisemitic insults.

In 2021, Germany's top court ruled that Naidoo could be referred to as an antisemite for his song texts. 

In 2022, he released a video statement apologizing for the "many mistakes" he had made over the years, including for "troubling statements," saying he regretted having ever made them.

Germany´s exports lose momentum

Emilio Reynoso

Germany's exports were down for the first time in months, the German Federal Statistical Office has confirmed.


For the first time since January,German exports fell by 0.8% due to lower demand from Europe and China.

Germany's shipments to China dropped by 9.5%, while exports to the Eurozone declined by 2.8%. Meanwhile, exports to the United States increased by 19%, continuing a slow recovery following President Donald Trump's tariffs.

Industrial production also declined in July, adding to growing concerns aboutfuel supplies in the country.

German students post dismal scores in latest PISA academic assessment

The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) released its 2025 PISA study in Paris on Tuesday and the results are shocking. 

According to the study, overall proficiency in reading, math and science has fallen significantly among 15-year-old pupils in 90 countries, with German pupils faring particularly poorly — posting their worst scores ever in the OECD's Program for International Student Assessment, which has tracked the academic performance since 2000.

"We are seeing historically low levels of proficiency," said Samuel Greiff of the Technical University of Munich. 

Released every three years, the 2025 assessment documented a major drop in German students' reading proficiency as well as significant dips in math and science skills since 2022.

"We aren't dramatically behind," said Greiff. "But we are not living up to our own standards as an education nation. We are not among the top performers." 

Among the factors contributing to Germany's poor scores were the education system's failings regarding early childhood education, teaching staffing, and digitalization.

Social media use was cited as a particular problem, leading Germany's Federal Commissioner for Drug Issues and Addiction Hendrik Streeck to ask, "How much scientific evidence do we need before we do something? Studies show that early, problematic social media consumption measurably impacts scholastic performance."

Roughly one-third of German students also told pollsters they considered school a "waste of time."

AfD victory prompts protests, opposition to party cooperation

Alternative for Germany's (AfD) landslide electoral victory in the eastern German state of Saxony-Anhalt on Sunday has sparked impromptu protests across the country, with several thousand people turning out in various cities.  

The largest of these took place in Berlin, where regional elections will take place on September 20, drawing an estimated 5,000 participants who exclaimed "fascism is not an alternative."

In a survey conducted by the polling outfit YouGov on Monday, September 7, nearly half of respondents spoke out against political parties working with the AfD to form a state government.

Of the 1,862 people aged 18 and over polled by YouGov, 47% opposed cooperation with AfD, whereas 38% looked favorably upon the proposition.  

Germany's domestic intelligence agency has classified the AfD's Saxony-Anhalt branch — which won nearly 44% of Sunday's vote but failed to secure a ruling majority — as "confirmed right-wing extremist."

Storage association warns Germany's gas reserves are dangerously low

Germany's gas and hydrogen storage association INES (Initiative for Energy Storage) on Tuesday said the country's gas storage facilities are at historically low levels as summer heat fades and winter approaches. 

In a paper presented in Berlin, INES said reserves were at 53% of capacity as of early September, the lowest they have been at this point in time over the past 15 years. 

The lobby group said levels could be brought up to 77% by November but only if input is dramatically increased. 

INES said 77% capacity would be enough to cover consumer and industry needs if winter temperatures remain normal but added, "77% capacity will not be enough in the event of extremely cold temperatures."       

With profit margins minimized by the wars in Russia and Iran, traders have little financial incentive to store natural gas according to INES. Storage facility operators are therefore calling on the German government to lower storage fees.

The Federal Ministry of Economy on the other hand, sees no reason for panic, adding that new LNG terminals off the German coast and pipeline deliveries from Norway can easily cover any potential shortages.
