Turkish authorities raid opposition Sozcu news channel

DW reporters with AFP, Reuters, AP
11/17/2026November 17, 2026

Turkish authorities have raided the Istanbul offices of Sozcu TV, halting broadcasts, appointing a trustee and opening an investigation. This marks another crackdown on pro-opposition television channels in Turkey.

Turkish police raided the Istanbul headquarters of the pro-opposition Sozcu TV early on Tuesday, arresting admins and installing a government trustee as the head, the group said on X.

The channel announced in a predawn post that police were searching the building under a court order.

What did Sozcu say about the raid by Turkish police?

"Police are searching the Sozcu building under a court order. Police have cordoned off the building," its official X account said in just before 5 a.m. (0200 GMT).

 

"A trustee has been appointed to Sozcu! Police are searching the building... There have been detentions," it said, with its journalists saying staff were not being allowed inside.

According to the media group, the trustee took control of the television channel and the news website but not the print newspaper.

After the raid, regular news broadcasts were stopped and a documentary was shown instead. 

At noon, the channel resumed its regular coverage, mentioning the raids only at the beginning of the broadcast. One of the channel's news presenters, Damla Dogan Tuncel, said on X that the channel had challenged the decision and was awaiting a court ruling.

"In the meantime, the broadcast would continue with normal news bulletins," she said.

'Terrorism' charges

The Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office said the action against Sozcu TV was part of an investigation into alleged breaches of Turkey's "anti-terror financing laws."

The prosecutor's office claimed that the channel had suspected links to a network that was led by US-based cleric Fethullah Gulen. The cleric, who died in 2024, was accused by Turkey of orchestrating a failed military coup in 2016.

Sozcu Media Group lawyer Ersan Barkin said the raids stemmed from a 2016 case that Turkey's Supreme Court had previously dismissed, finding no evidence of criminal intent or wrongdoing.

"This trustee decision stems from a file in which no offence was established," he told KRT TV.

Government silences last critical news channels

The move is the latest suppression of critical linear media in Turkey, coming a month after the police raided the country's most-watched news channel, Halk TV, and installed an administrator.

Since then, news broadcasts on the channel have remained on pause.

Another pro-opposition news channel TELE1 was also raided and seized by the government, while its owner Merdan Yanardag was jailed on espionage charges.

Over the last decade, President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's government has seized control of most of the media landscape, particularly television broadcasters.

While the critical voices were pushed onto the online space, Halk TV and Sozcu TV were prominent pro-opposition voices in TV.

No future in Turkey? Growing exodus of young Turks

Press freedom has suffered dramatically during Erdogan's 22 years in power.In the most recent Reporters Without Borders world press freedom index, Turkey ranked 163 of 180 countries.

Deutsche Welle correspondent Alican Uludag was also jailed and released this year on charges including insulting President Erdogan.

Opposition lashes out at the government

Opposition leader Ozgur Ozel, whose party was also raided and seized by a trustee, said "the government no longer has any tolerance left for even the slightest criticism."

"May our hearts wither if we surrender to these would-be dictators," he said on X.

Istanbul Mayor and Erdogan's jailed rival Ekrem Imamoglu said "the government's election fear is growing and growing."

"With courage, perseverance, and determination, people will continue the struggle," he said, from his blocked X account.
