Analysis: No more than crumbs of comfort on nuclear weapons

Richard Walker Chief International Editor

Donald Trump's big theme going into the three-way summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin was "denuclearization" — saying in his opening remarks that they were "uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth."

But there are no signs that that will happen.  

China has long resisted US calls for three-way talks on arms control, and Xi's reported call during the summit for a world without nuclear weapons was merely a reaffirmation of longstanding policy.  

In fact, as signatories of the global Nonproliferation Treaty, all nuclear states are committed to pursuing nuclear disarmament. So the fact that the final "Heads of State Declaration toward Peace" fails to indicate any steps towards disarmament could be read by critics as just the latest failure to take that seriously.  

China is engaged in a rapid buildup of its nuclear forces, from warheads to advanced theater missiles; Russia has made repeated threats to use nuclear weapons in its war in Ukraine; and the United States, despite Trump's calls for "denuclearization," is working on as many as four new theater missiles of its own to try to catch up with the other two in what could be the decisive nukes of this era.  

Neither should the Declaration's reaffirmation of the Reagan-Gorbachev mantra "a nuclear war can never be won and must never be fought" be seen as much source of comfort — all three states last retook this oath in January 2022, just weeks before Putin launched his war. 

And yet — the Declaration does have some crumbs of comfort for anyone seeking positive nuclear news. It says the three powers will "conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks." Possible areas for progress here would include commitments to notify other states when doing test launches of nuclear missiles (China has no such commitment), or keeping AI out of nuclear command and control (so far only the US and China have a very broad commitment that only humans should order a launch).  

This would be the lowest of low-hanging fruit — after all, does anyone want AI to launch nuclear weapons? – but an optimist might say, it would be better than nothing. Let's see.

European officials invited to Moscow to discuss involvement in Ukraine talks, says Putin

Russian President Vladimir Putin said European officials have been invited to Moscow to discuss the potential structure of talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine.

"The American side is urging us to agree to the involvement of the Europeans ... Through diplomatic channels, we suggested to the Europeans to come to Moscow. And after that we will decide in what format the talks can continue," Putin said at a press conference in China.

"We understand that discussing issues linked to security in Europe is pointless without the Europeans," Putin added.

For months, European politicians have emphasized that they should be present at any talks on the future of Ukraine. 

The Russian leader also said it would be unacceptable for there to be military cooperation between Ukraine and the EU and that included any potential deployment of troops to the country.

"Military cooperation in any form is, of course, a problem," Putin told reporters.

Allies of Ukraine, the "Coalition of the Willing," are meeting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy today in Berlin.

What do we know about agreements at the trilateral summit?

Timothy Jones

The highly anticipated three-way talks between US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on Thursday have ended.

Ahead of the talks, Trump said they would be about  "all problems that are being discussed in the world"

Here are quotes from the leaders and from a joint statement on the topics they addressed:

Nuclear arms

  • Trump: "The United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth and also to lessen the risk of those weapons being used."
  • Xi: "A nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. Every effort must be made to prevent nuclear war, adhere to the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons, and maintain global strategic stability."

 International Criminal Court and UN

  • In a joint statement reported by Chinese state media and the Kremlin, the leaders agreed that "the International Criminal Court and other similar institutions must not abuse their jurisdiction."
  • The leaders also "acknowledged the vital importance of the principle of state consent and the respect for (diplomatic) immunity enjoyed by state officials."
  • The statement added that all three countries were "committed" to advancing reform of the United Nations.

Energy

  • The joint statement said the countries "plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products."
  • It reaffirmed "the importance of the stable flow of energy."

Neither the war in Ukraine nor the conflict in Iran were directly mentioned at the talks, according to the Russian and Chinese accounts.

China and Russia report on 'peace declaration' from trilateral summit

Timothy Jones

Chinese state media and Russian officials have been summarizing a declaration issued by the US, Chinese and Russian presidents at their trilateral summit, which US President Donald Trump billed as history-making.

According to the accounts of what was dubbed a "peace declaration," the three leaders reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be ⁠won ​and must ​never be fought.

The three presidents of major nuclear powers are quoted as saying they would hold a dialogue on the reduction of nuclear risks and the prevention of nuclear proliferation.

The three men also "reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows," saying they plan to up the production and supply of refined petroleum products, according to the Chinese Xinhua news agency.

In addition, Chinese state media said the three had agreed that the International Criminal Court (ICC) — of which neither the US, China nor Russia are signatories — and similar institutions should not abuse their jurisdiction.

Putin himself is subject to an ICC arrest warrant for suspected war crimes in Ukraine.

The summary of the meeting did not mention the wars in Ukraine, triggered by Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion, or Iran, which began in late February with US-Israeli attacks on the country.

Both conflicts were expected to feature in the discussions.

Xi calls for nuclear-free world

Timothy Jones

Chinese President Xi Jinping has told his US and Russian counterparts at the three-way that nuclear weapons should be comprehensively banned and thoroughly destroyed, the state-run Xinhua news agency said.

11/19/2026November 19, 2026

Ukraine war being discussed at trilateral meeting — Trump

Timothy Jones

 US President Donald Trump said that the war in Ukraine would feature in the talks with his Russian and Chinese counterparts in China on Thursday.

"We are going to discuss the war between Russia and Ukraine," Trump said as the historic summit in Shenzhen got underway.

Russian President Vladimir Putin said all three countries had a shared "interest" to resolve the war in Ukraine, triggered by Moscow's 2022 full-scale invasion of its neighbor.

"Our countries have many converging interests when it comes to resolving regional conflicts, including, of course, the conflict in Ukraine," Putin said in opening remarks.

He added they had a "shared responsibility for maintaining international peace and security" and that closed-door discussions would also touch on food and energy security.

The global security of both food and energy has been weakened both by the war in Ukraine and the conflict in Iran, unleashed by US-Israel attacks on the Islamic Republic at the end of February.

Trilateral meeting to cover all the world's problems — Trump

Timothy Jones

As the trilateral summit got underway, US President Donald Trump said he would be talking about "all problems that are being discussed in the world."

Among other things, he said, the talks would cover the issue of nuclear arms.

"The United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth and also to lessen the risk of those weapons being used," he said.

Both Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin emphasized a focus on peace in the world.

"We will be having discussions on the theme of promoting world peace," Xi said, adding that China, the US and Russia should "jointly safeguard" international peace.

Putin echoed Xi's statement, saying that the three countries were "the victors in WWII, and we hold joint responsibility for maintaining international peace."

Japan wants 'constructive and stable relationship' with China

Timothy Jones

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said on Thursday that she intends to build a "constructive and stable relationship" with Beijing, in comments made at a time when Japan-China relations are at a low point over a remark she made on Taiwan.

"We intend to comprehensively advance our strategic and mutually beneficial relationship ... pursuing dialogue to safeguard the national interests of both sides, and build a constructive and stable relationship," Takaichi said at a news conference on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

She said that Japan's position on Taiwan had not changed from that stated in its 1972 ‌Japan-China Joint Communique, which established ‌diplomatic ​relations between Tokyo and Beijing and severed those previously held with Taiwan.

China considers the democractically-run Taiwan part of its sovereign territory and has threatened to take it by force if necessary.

Beijing reacted angrily after Takaichi said in November 2025 that a Taiwan Strait crisis could threaten Japan's survival and thus justified a self-defense response.

Takaichi's welcome to the APEC summit was very low-key, which has been seen by observers as a sign that Beijing's ire at the remark has not subsided.

Unlike other world leaders, including US President Donald Trump, Takaichi also did not hold a bilateral meeting ​with Chinese ⁠President Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the summit in Shenzhen.

Xi says China-US relations have remained 'stable and improved' in bilateral talks with Trump

Mahima Kapoor

Chinese President Xi said he was committed to working with President Trump to steer ties between the two superpowers to the "correct course," after a brief bilateral meeting with the US President ahead of the trilateral talks. 

"During this period, China-US relations have remained generally stable and improved, bringing tangible benefits to the people of both countries and injecting stability and certainty into the world," Xi told Trump at their third meeting this year.

"We have ⁠established ​a new ​definition of a constructive China-US ​relationship of strategic ‌stability, giving it new substance based ​on ⁠respect, fairness and reciprocity," Xi ⁠said.

The leaders reportedly also discussed the possibility of China buying US farm goods, the Iran war and Russia's war in Ukraine. 

"We're looking to both help with respect to the war with Russia and Ukraine. I'd like to see it ended, I think I can speak for both of us, very much," Trump said after the meeting concluded.

Trump calls Putin 'my friend' after bilateral talks

Mahima Kapoor

Presidents Trump and Putin have concluded bilateral talks that lasted around 40 minutes.

"Wonderful things can come out of this — or at least start," Trump said after joining Putin for the one-on-one meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit, calling Putin "my friend."

"We've had a great relationship for many years," Trump said after shaking hands with his Russian counterpart. 

The two last met in Alaska last year, but the warmer — in comparison to the previous administration — relationship between Trump and Putin has so far failed to bring an end to the war in Ukraine, despite a pledge by the US president to end it on "day one" of his second term in office.

Meanwhile, Russian news agency TASS reported that the leaders discussed the war in Ukraine, US-Russia bilateral ties and the upcoming trilateral meeting.

Trump: US opposes 'any form of globalist regulation' of AI

Mahima Kapoor

The United States is against "globalist regulation" of artificial intelligence in any form, President Trump said on Thursday, ahead of the trilateral talks with China's Xi and Russia's Putin. 

"It's super intelligence [SI], but we have to watch it," he said, using the term mandated by his administration to refer to AI. Trump hails great 'friendship' with Xi

Mahima Kapoor | Felix Tamsut

US President Trump highlighted the pomp surrounding his arrival in China, which stood in contrast to the more muted reception received by other leaders.

Chinese President Xi greeted Trump at the airport, reciprocating his counterpart's gesture at Joint Base Andrews in September, when the two last met.

This was the first time that Xi greeted a world leader personally at the airport since he became president.

"It's a friendship relationship. It's a respect relationship," Trump told Fox News about his Chinese counterpart when asked what he hoped to achieve from the trilateral meeting.

"And not only that, the fanfare, the beauty, the pageantry, the thousands of people, the young children, the soldiers, the whole thing. It ​was great respect, and I appreciate that. That's a big win for us. It's a big win for China too."

Trump also said he was "looking forward" to joint talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

The US president spoke about the prospect of reaching an agreement on China's nuclear arsenal as well.

"Look, we have 4,000 nuclear weapons. Russia has 3,800 nuclear weapons. China now has four or five hundred, but they're going to catch [up]" Trump said.
