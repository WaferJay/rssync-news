Delhi spent less than half of its tuberculosis budget in 2025

The Delhi government used less than half of its budget under the National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP) last year, according to official records.

Delhi spent only 47.4% of the total funds allocated in 2025 to fight Tuberculosis.

Only Rs 51.32 crore (about €4.9 million) of the Rs 108.27 crore approved under the NTEP was utilised, leaving Rs 56.95 crore unspent, according to the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) report of 2025-26.

The funds were underused even though 112,977 TB cases were notified in 2025 in the capital, according to the Delhi State Health Mission (DSHS).

Explaining the discrepancy, a Ministry of Health official told The Indian Express that under the program, beneficiaries receive funds directly and "gaps in digitisation and beneficiary identification contributed to this shortfall in fund utilisation."

'First Diwali without Naxal terror,' Modi says in Chhattisgarh

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Sunday joined security forces deployed in the central state of Chhattisgarh to celebrate Diwali. 

The state has long been affected by a Maoist-inspired Naxal insurgency.

Celebrating Diwali with security forces in Chhattisgarh's Bastar, Modi said, "Because of your sacrifices, we have put an end to the terror of Naxalism here."

"This is the first Diwali where every single district in the country is free from Naxalism. This is the first Diwali where crores of our citizens have overcome the fear of Naxalism," he said.

Modi made an indirect reference to the youth protests that took place in the past few weeks demanding the removal of election chief Gyanesh Kumar.

Modi called the protesters "Dimagi naxals" (Intellectual naxals) and said, "While armed Naxalites have been wiped out from the forests, 'Dimagi Naxals' have taken control of several organisations."

"Even today, they are inciting people against the country's Constitution and democracy. We must identify them, defeat them and ensure the security of the nation," he said.

Modi's remarks come after his government announced in March that the country has been freed from Naxal groups after several Maoist leaders and cadres were killed in operations by India's security forces.

Skip next section Sonam Wangchuk accuses Ladakh governor of smear campaign
11/08/2026November 8, 2026

Sonam Wangchuk accuses Ladakh governor of smear campaign

Activist Sonam Wangchuk has said he is the subject of a witch-hunt by the Ladakh Lieutenant Governor's office, which he accused of targeting him in a smear campaign for standing with the youth of the country and participating in protests.

"I am being continuously witch-hunted and defamed and our schools are being harassed in every possible way by the LG administration in Ladakh," said Wangchuk, who is a member of Leh Apex Body (LAB), a representative body of the people of Ladakh.

In a video message on Saturday to Indian President Droupadi Murmu, Wangchuk asked her to intervene, saying, "Madam President, for the last six years. We don't have rights like in the rest of the country or democracy."

In 2019, Ladakh lost the special status it shared with the former state of Jammu and Kashmir under Article 370 and became a separate Union Territory. The change meant that the Indian central government in New Delhi now directly governs the Himalayan region.

Since 2019, several political and civil society groups in Ladakh have been demanding greater autonomy and, in many cases, statehood.

Wangchuk said two social media pages, Ladakh Lens 1 and Ladakh Vision 1, surfaced on October 14 and that the former was allegedly created to defame him through "unfounded allegations," while the latter was aimed at "praising the Lieutenant Governor's work" in Ladakh.

The Ladakh administration has not responded to the allegations so far.

Wangchuk also accused the Ladakh administration of targeting institutes like his that have "earned national and international repute."

Earlier this month, the Ladakh administration directed the Himalayan Institute of Alternative Learning (HIAL), led by Wangchuk and his wife Gitanjali J. Angmo, to halt activities at its Phyang-based school until the Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh High Court issues further notice.

Wangchuk has questioned the approvals being demanded by the administration.

Skip next section Delhi most polluted city in world on Diwali eve
11/08/2026November 8, 2026

Delhi most polluted city in world on Diwali eve

Delhi awoke Sunday morning enveloped in smog with poor visibility after the previous night saw the worst air quality for Diwali eve in 10 years.

India's Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) said the Indian capital had a "very poor" air quality index (AQI) of 362 on Saturday. The worst polluted pre-Diwali day was in 2016, when the AQI hit 404 on October 29, according to the CPCB.

The air quality is forecast to turn "severe" on Sunday, with Diwali celebrations adding to the pollution.

Swiss monitor IQAir, meanwhile, ranked Delhi as the world's worst polluted major city on Saturday.

While vehicular emissions and stubble burning in neighboring states of Punjab and Haryana are major contributors to air pollution, Diwali celebrations, which involve large-scale burning of firecrackers, add to the pollution in the city.

The government had already ordered schools in and ​around Delhi to offer online classes, and ​encouraged people to work from home ahead of ⁠the Hindu festival of light.

Skip next section Madhya Pradesh: Sanitation workers call off three-day strike
11/07/2026November 7, 2026

Madhya Pradesh: Sanitation workers call off three-day strike

Sanitation workers in state capital Bhopal ended their indefinite strike after three days following late-night talks with Chief Minister Mohan Yadav, according to Indian news agency Press Trust of India.

According to the sanitation workers' union, the breakthrough came after the state government agreed to initiate the regularization process for workers employed for 25 days a month, a move which will allow a pay hike.

According to local media, the sanitation workers had four major demands: a pay rise, compassionate appointments, regularization of jobs and an end to the short-term gig worker contract system. Their primary demand is an increase in the monthly wage from Rs 10,500 (about $111) to Rs 25,000.

The protest had left Bhopal, which was named the cleanest state capital in India in 2023, littered with heaps of uncollected garbage.

The workers claimed that more than 100,000 workers participated in the agitation across Madhya Pradesh.

Mohammed Shami retires from Test cricket

One of India’s most successful fast bowlers, Mohammed Shami, has announced his retirement from Test cricket.

The move brings his 13-year career to an end, but he will continue to play white ball formats.

The 36-year-old cricketer announced in an Instagram video that he was ending his career in whites.

"I am bidding farewell to the white jersey, but never to the memories associated with it. When I wore India's Test jersey for the first time, I had only one dream — to do something for my country. Since then, every wicket, every struggle, every injury, and every comeback has taught me something," Shami said.

Shami made his Test debut against the West Indies at the Eden Gardens on November 6, 2013. He finished with 229 wickets at an average of 27.71, including six five-wicket hauls.

His international red ball journey spans 229 wickets from 64 matches, making him the sixth most successful pacer in Tests for the country behind Kapil Dev (434), Zaheer Khan, Ishant Sharma (both 311), Javagal Srinath (236), Jasprit Bumrah (234).

Shami last played for India in Tests in the World Test Championship final against Australia at The Oval in 2023.

Since then, injuries have disrupted his career, but he remained an important part of India's white-ball setup.

Shami clarified that his retirement would not mark the end of his playing career. He said he would continue to play white-ball cricket and could also turn out in four-day matches occasionally.

X says takedown orders against free speech groups were fake

Social media platform X said that police orders it received to remove online accounts of two of India's most ​prominent free speech groups were fake. 

Lawyers representing X told the court that someone impersonating Delhi police had sent the emails.

X received orders through its online reporting form in the name of a ​senior Delhi police officer seeking removal of the online accounts of free speech groups, the Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF) and the Software Freedom Law Centre, ⁠India (SFLC.in). 

The orders claimed the groups had defamed the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh, parent organization of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Hindu nationalist Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).

After an internal investigation, the social media company's lawyers on Friday told the Delhi High Court that, "It ​turns out this was somebody playing an impostor, somebody impersonating Delhi police who has sent this."

The orders had sparked an uproar on social media this week and were challenged for their legality by Amnesty and India's main opposition party.

India's top court says vehicle emissions a main cause of Delhi's low air quality

The Supreme Court of India on Friday said that vehicular pollution is a major contributor to the deteriorating air quality in Delhi and questioned the effectiveness of the government's measures to curb it.

The bench, comprising Chief Justice of India (CJI) Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana, opened the proceedings with a jibe saying, "I hope you all are enjoying the weather in Delhi."

This week, Delhi's Air Quality Index (AQI) has remained above the "hazardous" 300 mark.

Air quality in the capital regularly plummets at the outset of winter, as weather conditions help trap pollutants.

"A major cause of the pollution is vehicular pollution. Look at the roads in Delhi, look at the roads connecting Delhi to the NCR (the National Capital Region)… the kind of bumper-to-bumper traffic… that's the major cause," the CJI said.

The court examined the steps taken by the government to control the rising levels of air pollution.

Expressing concerns over the failure of measures to curb air pollution in Delhi over the years, the court said, "To think we can achieve air quality of less than 100 AQI in Delhi during winters is utopian when 'very poor' and 'severe' have become the new normal."

On stubble burning, the court said farmers should be given economic incentives and practical alternatives.

The court also encouraged responsible celebrations during festivals, particularly Diwali, where firecrackers being set off adds to the air pollution.

"Why don't we revert to our original customs?" the CJI said, adding celebrations should be accompanied by a sense of responsibility towards the environment.
