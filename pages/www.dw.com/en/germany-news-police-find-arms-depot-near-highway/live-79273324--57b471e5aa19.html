Wadephul and Söder back Merz

Embattled German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has received public backing from two senior conservative allies, Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul (CDU) and Bavarian state premier Markus Söder (CSU), ahead of a key party meeting in Berlin on Sunday.

"We have been elected for a full parliament, we will fulfill our responsibility in this parliament, and we will do it under this chancellor," insisted Wadephul after taking part in a meeting of the Bavarian state parliament in Munich on Tuesday.

"That's what he was elected for," he said of Merz. "I voted for him. We voted for him. I enjoy working in his cabinet and I am determined to continue doing so."

Bavarian state premier Söder, who chaired the cabinet meeting, also called for "togetherness" and "strength" in support of the chancellor, saying:

"It would be a real problem if, amid all the complications of these times in which we are living, with hybrid attacks on our democracy, Germany were to falter."

CDU calls crisis meeting as pressure mounts on Merz

The leadership of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's center-right Christian Democratic Union (CDU) is to hold a crisis meeting in Berlin on election night amid mounting speculation over the chancellor's political future.

The CDU's executive committee will meet under Merz's leadership on Sunday evening to assess the party's latest state election results and discuss its next steps, a party spokeswoman said. A meeting of the wider federal party leadership is due to follow on Monday.

Merz is expected to seek a clear show of support from senior CDU figures as criticism of his leadership intensifies.

Could Merz seek a confidence vote?

One option reportedly under consideration is for Merz to seek an explicit declaration of confidence from the party leadership.

There has been speculation that influential CDU politicians at state level could seek to oust the increasingly unpopular chancellor. A declaration of support could help Merz head off such a challenge.

The CDU executive committee is the party's most senior inner leadership body and includes all CDU state premiers.

Political scientist Oliver Lembcke of Ruhr University Bochum said preparations at CDU headquarters suggested Merz himself now regarded an early end to his chancellorship as a realistic possibility.

"That is why Merz is trying to rally the influential state premiers behind him in particular," Lembcke told the AFP news agency.

"If they do not demonstratively express their confidence in him after the elections, his situation will become very difficult."

Merz determined to continue despite plummeting ratings

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he is is determined to serve out his full four-year term despite sinking ratings and growing pressure following his Christian Democratic Union (CDU)'s recent electoral defeat in the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt.

"I was elected for four years and I want to use this time to solve problems," he said on Tuesday, insisting: "We have to prove that this ​can be achieved with ‌this coalition."

Merz said he intended to pursue further reforms alongside the CDU's coalition partner, the Social Democrats (SPD). "This is not possible with other majorities in parliament," he said.

Nationally, the conservative CDU and its Bavarian sister party, the Christian Social Union (CSU), ​are polling at between 18% and 20%, far below their result in last year's federal election ​and significantly behind the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD).

This week, a new INSA poll found that a majority of CDU supporters and 78% of the wider public no longer believe Merz is the right person for the job.

Merz: Government will act to combat fuel prices

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said on Tuesday that his government would take action to protect consumers from rising fuel prices (seen entry below).

"We will respond to profiteering," he told a meeting of business leaders in Berlin, without specifying precisely how.

"I am of the opinion that we must act," he said, adding that discussions between the federal government and state governments had already taken place. "You can rest assured that we will be making suggestions soon."

Gas prices in Germany continue to rise with standard unleaded Super E10 currently costing an average of €2.286 per liter and diesel at €2.412 per liter – only a few cents below the all-time record.

German coalition split on how to tackle soaring fuel prices

Kieran Burke Editor

Divisions are opening up in Germany's government over current surging fuel prices, with the center-left Social Democrats (SPD) accusing conservative Economy Minister Katherina Reiche of inaction.

"We've had enough," the SPD's general secretary, Tim Klüssendorf, told the news portal t-online.

"Millions of people in our country are still dealing with completely excessive fuel prices, and the responsible minister, Katherina Reiche, is watching by while oil giants make record profits at the expense of the public," Klüssendorf said. "We urgently need measures against extortionate fuel prices."

What are the SPD's proposals?

The SPD is calling for three measures to bring down the high fuel prices.

They include:

  • a price cap based on those in place in Belgium and Luxembourg
  •  a tax on excessive profits made by oil companies
  •  transparency on how prices emerge

What have the conservatives suggested?

However, Reiche has rejected the idea of a price cap, instead proposing direct payments to those affected by the fuel prices.

"This would be one way of providing rapid relief, but it would need to be targeted specifically at those income groups that pay little or no tax," she said on Monday.

Economic expert warns on fuel tax cuts

Earlier this year, the conservative-led government put in place a two-month fuel tax cut to relieve motorists.

But the Kiel Institute for the World Economy (IfW) on Tuesday cautioned against repeating such a measure.

IFW economist Klaus-Jürgen Gern told Deutschlandfunk radio that a general fuel tax cut was counterproductive.

"It supports people who don't need it. And in the end, there is a shortfall in public funds, where there are actually more important things to finance. There is then a shortfall for investment, and a shortfall for other important social spending," he said.

Fuel prices soaring

The row comes as gasoline reached a new high of €2.273 ($2.62) per liter ($9.92 per US gallon) on Sunday according to average national figures from the ADAC automobile club.

The ADAC said diesel reached €2.40 per liter on Sunday, just some 4 cents under the record price hit in April as a consequence of the US-Israeli war with Iran, which is the main factor driving the rise in fuel prices throughout the world.

Far-right AfD remains strongest party in new survey

Emilio Reynoso

The Alternative for Germany (AfD) remains the party with the most nationwide support, according to a YouGov poll published on Tuesday.

The survey comes after the nationalist party achieved a resounding victory in elections in the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt earlier in September.

In the YouGov poll, which was conducted between Friday and Monday, the AfD scored 29%, gaining one percentage point compared with August.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz's conservative Christian Democratic Union (CDU) and its Bavarian sister party, the Christian Social Union (CSU), fell to 18%, a record low.

Support for the Greens rose to 16%, while the Social Democrats, the junior partner in the governing coalition with the CDU/CSU, gained one point, reaching 13%.

The Left party were at 10%, losing two points. The Free Democrats (FDP) remained at 5%, and the populist Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance (BSW) rose to 4%.

The majority of those surveyed by YouGov (59%) said they consider the AfD to be a far-right party, while 30% disagree. This figure has changed very little since May 2025.

However, attitudes towards the so-called "firewall," a policy under which traditional parties refuse to cooperate with the AfD, have changed considerably. The survey found that 51% of respondents believe that cooperation with the AfD should not be categorically ruled out — up from 44% in August.

Berlin woman charged with supporting 'IS'

Berlin state prosecutors have charged a 35-year-old German woman with supporting the "Islamic State" (IS) terror organization.

The woman is accused of having traveled via Istanbul in Turkey to Syria in December 2014 to join the group there.

Prosecutors allege that she followed her husband, who wanted to fight for the extremist group.

They said she had lived with him in the Syrian town of Manbij, where she supported his IS activities.

After leaving the town in February 2015 and separating from her husband, she is said to have lived in IS-run women's accommodation.

Support for 'IS' ideology

During this time, she allegedly convinced a female friend in Berlin to come to Syria to support IS. The friend is said to have later been killed fighting with the group.

The woman is also said to have declared her willingness to take part in suicide attacks.

After being captured by Kurdish armed forces in 2019 and spending some time in a camp near the border to Iraq, she allegedly escaped to Turkey with the aid of her ex-husband, living there under a false name until April 2026.

The woman was arrested at Berlin Airport in April this year upon her return to Germany.

09/15/2026September 15, 2026

Defense Minister Pistorius heads to US for talks

German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius is on his way to the United States for a meeting with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth at the Pentagon.

Before setting off on his trip, Pistorius mentioned Germany's order of US-made F-35 stealth jets, which are set to replace the German Air Force's aging Tornado combat planes.

On Friday, he will visit the manufacturer of the planes, Lockheed Martin in Fort Worth, Texas, where the first of the aircraft will be officially handed over.

Pistorius said another focus of the talks could be plans to make guided missiles for the Patriot air defense system in Germany.

The German government also intends to buy Tomahawk cruise missiles in the US, having reached a preliminary agreement on a deal on the sidelines of the NATO summit in Ankara in July.

Police find arms stash in Rhineland-Palatinate

Police have found a cache of weapons and ammunition near a major country road in the western state of Rhineland-Palatinate but no clues as to who put it there or why.

The depot contained several rifles, shotguns and pistols, in addition to a submachine gun, a flare gun and several thousands of rounds for various weapons, police in Ludwigshafen said.

The find was made near a car park on a road in Speyer on Monday, they said.
