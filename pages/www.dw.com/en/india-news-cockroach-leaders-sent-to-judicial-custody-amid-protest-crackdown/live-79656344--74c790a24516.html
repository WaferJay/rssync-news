Air India Frankfurt-Delhi flight diverted after bomb threat

An Air India Aircraft flying from Frankfurt to Delhi was diverted to the Indian city of Jaipur on Wednesday, following a bomb threat, news agency Press Trust of India reported. 

The Boeing aircraft made an emergency landing at the Jaipur International Airport and was immediately escorted to a designated isolation bay. Airport authorities alerted the police about the emergency landing and the bomb threat. 

Meanwhile, Indian media reported that the airplane's pilot issued the emergency code of '7500,' the internationally recognized aviation code for hijacking or unlawful interference. The aircraft has since been cleared. 

India's aviation regulator is currently probing the incident, Hindustan Times reported citing sources.

Boat capsizes in Bihar, killing 13

A boat capsized in the river Ganges in the eastern state of Bihar, drowning at least 13 people, authorities said on Wednesday.

News agency Press Trust of India reported that all of the deceased were children under the age of 15.  At least six others on board were rescued. 

The boat overturned on Tuesday night while carrying passengers to a feast in a neighboring village across the river, authorities told local media.

Rescue operators have retrieved the vessel and are conducting an investigation. 

Several regional and national leaders expressed their grief over the incident. Prime Minister Narendra Modi said it was "deeply painful," offering his condolences to the bereaved families.

"I pray for the speedy recovery of all those injured. The local administration is assisting those affected,"  Modi said through his office's account on X. 

Bihar Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary said the News was "heartbreaking and soul-wrenching."

"Instructions have been given to the administration to swiftly complete relief and rescue operations and ensure better treatment for the injured," he said in a post.

Boat accidents are a common occurrence in India where safety measures are deficient.

Jantar Mantar: What to know about Delhi's delisted protest site?

Jantar Mantar, in central Delhi, is an 18th century open-air astronomical observatory. 

For over three decades, a stretch of road leading up to it was where Indians came to be heard, press their demands and hold those in power accountable. That was until last week when the Delhi Police delisted the iconic venue as a protest site. 

What sparked the latest protests?

The move came days after a confrontation between thousands of police personnel and anti-government protesters led by India's Cockroach Janta Party (CJP).

The group, along with student organizations and activists, mobilized thousands to protest against a revision in electoral rolls which reportedly deleted 130 million voters.

While those deleted include dead and duplicate voters, critics argue the staggering number likely includes a vast number of eligible voters. India's poll body and the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party party have denied this.

Jantar Mantar has been the central rallying location for protesters demanding election Chief Gyanesh Kumar's resignation for overseeing the revision.

This is the same location that acted as a home base for the CJP-led youth protests in July over an exam scandal, which resulted in the removal of India's former education minister.

Why is delisting Jantar Mantar controversial?

Now, instead of Jantar Mantar, authorities have designated three alternative venues in Delhi. Campaigners argue those venues will reduce visibility and proximity to lawmakers as Jantar Mantar was surrounded by the nation's biggest institutions. 

"Jantar Mantar is in the centre of the town, it is connected, it is next to parliament... it was a very important space for all protests," human rights activist Shabnam Hashmi told AFP. "This is nothing but a move to curb dissent," he said.

CJP leaders Ashutosh Ranka and Saurav Das were among about a dozen people who jumped barricades on Tuesday to enter the grounds of Jantar Mantar, despite the delisting a day earlier. 

An Indian court has now sent Das and Ranka to five days in judicial custody over trespassing and other violations.

Cockroach party conveners Ranka, Das sent to 5 days in judicial custody

Mahima Kapoor

Three leaders of India's Cockroach movement — Co-Conveners Saurav Das, Ashutosh Ranka and organizer Ajinkya Shinde — have been formally charged with multiple violations by authorities in India's national capital.

It all comes amid their protest against India's election chief over mass deletion of voter names from electoral rolls. 

Indian authorities crack down on 'Cockroach' protest

The trio was detained early Tuesday after they allegedly "sneaked" past police barricades to enter the grounds of Jantar Mantar in central Delhi, which until recently was a designated protest site. 

News agency PTI, citing sources, reported that police have now formally charged them with trespassing, violation of prohibitory orders, breach of a peace bond and obstructing a public servant from discharging officials duties. 

Meanwhile, Cockroach Janta Party's Founder Abhijeet Dipke said Das and Ranka had been "sent to five days judicial custody." 

"Their fault? Unfurling tricolor at Jantar Mantar," Dipke wrote on X on Wednesday morning, referring to the national flag. 

"Show some guts, Narendra Modi. If you're so determined to crush dissent, have the courage to officially declare an Emergency instead of hiding behind these cowardly tactics," he said.

Dipke's post suggests the trio have already been produced in front of a magistrate. Under Indian law, a person arrested by police has to appear in court within 24 hours of the arrest. One can only be sent to judicial remand by a judge. 

Defending advocate accuses police of making false claims in court

Advocate Abhisht Hela, who is representing Das and Ranka, questioned the grounds for sending the two into judicial remand and accused the police of making false claims in court, Times of India reported. 

"They have been sent to five-day judicial custody because of the presumption of the police that they can commit a crime. Police lied in court that they had given a call urging people to come there," Hela said, adding that no such call was made.

Meanwhile, Shinde, who seems to have been released, told reporters that the government was misusing its powers to curtail people's rights. "If the government thinks that sending our two leaders, Ashutosh and Saurav, to jail for five days will frighten them, it is absolutely mistaken," Shinde said, as per PTI.

He further called on "everyone" to "come together to bring an end to this dictatorial government."
