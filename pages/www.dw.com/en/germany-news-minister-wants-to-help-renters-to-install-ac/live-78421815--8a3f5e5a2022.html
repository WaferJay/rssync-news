Christian Democrat regional leader joins Tinder in heat of election campaign

Struggling in the polls a month before state elections, the Christian Democrats' (CDU) candidate to govern Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania is trying to woo back voters in an unusual campaign battleground — the dating app Tinder. 

Daniel Peters has copped some criticism from rival parties but argues that it's little different to his using social media networks like Instagram and Facebook. 

"I'm interested in your life. Let's discuss what needs to improve," Peters writes where people would normally seek to find a potential partner rather than a preferred politician. 

For the avoidance of doubt, a party spokesman in the northeastern state stressed that the 45-year-old "is happily married and is not looking for dates." 

Peters said that his presence on Tinder was prompting engagement, saying replies lay in three-figure territory and were generally positive. 

"The format is seen as being entertaining and informative," he said. 

Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania is one of three states set to vote in a new regional parliament next month. Polls suggest the CDU is seriously struggling in the state, which has never exactly been a happy hunting ground for it. 

Most surveys put the center-right party somewhere in the region of 10% support, compared to 13.3% in 2021. The AfD appears set to become the most popular party in the state, overtaking the Social Democrats (SPD) and claiming first place but falling well short of the majority it would need to govern. 

As major German parties maintain a policy of non-cooperation with the AfD — known as the "firewall" — the far-right party is unlikely to form government in the state.

The vote takes place on September 20.

Fuel prices continue climbing, ADAC says costs unjustified

The prices at fuel pumps in Germany are still climbing according to the latest averages collated by the ADAC motoring club. 

The price of E10 gasoline or petrol reached a national average of just over €2.16 per liter on Wednesday. That equates to $9.54 per US gallon. It's a slight increase, just under 4 cents per liter on the previous Wednesday. 

Diesel, meanwhile, climbed more sharply in the past week, rising by just over 5 cents per liter. 

It reached an average price of just over €2.25 per liter, or $9.95 per US gallon. 

Regional variations are fairly stark at present, as the problems transporting fuel on the Rhine River amid its low levels create delivery difficulties in western states like North Rhine-Westphalia and Hessen. 

The ADAC, however, said it believed that neither the shipping issues in the Strait of Hormuz nor the summer drought could explain the prices. 

"In the view of the ADAC, the fuel prices in Germany remain on a level that is too high," the organization, which often criticizes petrol pricing practices, said. "While the historically low water levels on the Rhine and the transport difficulties that causes and the slight increase in oil prices explain a part of the fuel cost rises, they are not sufficient to fully explain the current price levels. Filling up is still too expensive."

Eurowings looks to restart Dubai flights after months

German airline Eurowings plans to restart flights to Dubai in the United Arab Emirates, after a hiatus of almost six months as a result of the US war with Iran that has impacted the wider Persian Gulf region. 

The airline made the announcement on Wednesday, albeit only for a probable restart on October 25, by which point almost eight months will have elapsed. 

The routes from Berlin, Stuttgart and Cologne/Bonn to Dubai were all suspecnded soon after the joint US-Israeli attack on Iran at the end of February. Iran has targeted sites in Gulf countries over the course of the war, including in the UAE, saying it is in retaliation to the US and Israeli bombardment.

The Lufthansa subsidiary, typically seen as a budget alternative, also announced that from December 1, new premium business seats would be available on Dubai flights. 

For the first time, except on long-distance routes, the airline will use a more spacious layout with just four seats per row for these seats. 

Eurowings said it was extending this concept to all eight of its A320neo planes, meaning that the new seats will also be available on flights to London, Mallorca and Malaga.

NRW's interior minister visits Monschau amid Belgian border wildfire

Saim Dušan Inayatullah Editor

The interior minister of Germany's western state of North Rhine-Westphalia (NRW), Herbert Reul, has visited the border town of Monschau, which remains on alert amid the wildfire a short distance away in Belgium's High Fens nature reserve.

Some 30 residents in the most at-risk parts of the town are still under evacuation orders. 

Local officials in Belgium like the Liege regional governor however reported "positive developments" fighting the 3,000-hectare blaze on Wednesday amid sporadic light rains and cooler temperatures. 

Reul, meanwhile, warned against rumors and speculation about the cause of the fire during his visit to Monschau. He was speaking as unsubstantiated allegations of wild campers triggering the vast fire circulated. 

"There's no point to it," Reul said when asked about speculating on the cause. He said it would require thorough investigation, and then prosecution if applicable. 

If investigations did not yield fruit, he said, the most important lesson was for people not to behave irresponsibly in the woods, not to drop cigarette butts or cook using hot coals. 

The majority of forest fires are ultimately man-made, with very few naturally occurring sources of heat (other than lightning strikes) sufficient to spark fires amid foliage. Dry ground, high temperatures and winds help the fires to spread once they have begun.

Ever more foreign qualifications recognized in Germany — Destatis

Germany is accepting the qualifications of a growing number of foreign professionals, the Federal Statistical Office (Destatis) reported on Wednesday.

It said authorities had approved 86,600 applications last year, almost 10% more than in 2024.

That rise is, however, smaller than in 2024 and 2023, which saw the number of approvals increase by 21% and 25% respectively.

As in previous years, most of those accepted in 2025 came from Turkey (9,800), accounting for 11% of overall approvals. Around 8,300 recognitions from Ukraine were also reported.

Most of the recognized qualifications were in medical professions, as was also the case in 2024.

Nursing professionals had 32,000 applications approved, followed by doctors (13,900).

There were also 4,600 acceptances for engineers, meaning that those three professions together made up more than half (58%) of recognitions.

A number of sectors in Germany are relying ever more on foreign professionals as the birth rate falls and the population ages.

Investigation bureau gives details on near-accident in Munich

Germany's Federal Bureau of Aircraft Accident Investigation (BFU) has said the causes of a near-accident at the weekend involving a Vietnam Airlines aircraft were still unclear but presented details on the incident.

In a statement issued late on Tuesday, the BFU said the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner started its takeoff at Munich Airport at 1:57 pm (1157 GMT) on Saturday and was late getting into the air.

It said the tail touched the ground just before lift-off and that tire marks had been found at the end of the runway that were probably from the aircraft.

The plane eventually turned around, landing back at Munich Airport at 3:58 p.m.

The agency said its experts were currently analyzing the plane's flight recorders and that authorities in Vietnam and the US were also participating in the investigation.

"The investigation does not serve to establish blame, liability or claims," the authority said, but rather was intended to help prevent any such accidents and incidents in future.

Experts have said that the 270 passengers and crew on board the plane narrowly escaped disaster.

The aircraft had great difficulty taking off despite the pilot's efforts to pull the nose up.

When it landed two hours later, smoke was visible from the undercarriage and tires had burst. Passengers and crew were uninjured.

Tenants should be able to install heat protection more easily — justice minister

Laws on rented accommodation could potentially be changed to allow tenants more freedom in adapting their apartments to cope with heat ways, Federal Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig has said.

"Conventional legal policy can also make a contribution to heat protection," she told the daily Rheinische Post in an interview published on Wednesday morning.

"I am thinking about tenancy laws in particular," she said. "I can imagine, for example, that we make it easier for tenants to equip their apartments with air conditioning or sun shades."

At present, tenants have no legal right to install built-in air conditioning devices or external shades but need permission from their rental owner.

Hubig also spoke out in favor of the proposal made by her Social Democratic Party to enshrine climate protection in the German constitution, or Basic Law, as a joint obligation of both the federal government and state administrations. Currently, the primary responsibility for heat protections lies with the states and municipalities.

"This would make it easier for the government to give states and municipalities sustained support in heat-protection measures," she said. 

Germany, like much of ‌Europe, has experienced ⁠successive ⁠heat waves this summer with temperatures repeatedly approaching 40 degrees Celsius (104 degrees Fahrenheit). This has reignited public debate over the use of air conditioners, which remain rare in the country.

Runaway sheep goes through bus wash

A German sheep has probably had the experience of its life after it boarded an empty bus that was then driven through a bus wash.

The sheep had escaped from its home in the western town of Lüdinghausen and gone aboard the bus at the local bus depot, police said on Tuesday.

According to the transport company RVM, the sheep entered the bus as it was about to be cleaned in the depot's wash hall, causing officers to be called to the scene.

The police then escorted the vehicle as it went through the wash to prevent the sheep escaping, as the bus was already in such a position that it could not back out of the facility.

The owner then collected the animal after what must have been a confusing ride for it.

RVM said the sheep was one of several that had run across the depot grounds during the incident a few days ago.

Skip next section Defense startup CEO living under ground due to Russian plots — reports
08/19/2026August 19, 2026

Defense startup CEO living under ground due to Russian plots — reports

The head of a German defense startup that is supplying attack drones to Ukraine as it defends itself against invading Russian troops has been in hiding for months after warnings that he was at risk of his life from Russian agents, reports said on Tuesday.

Donaustahl CEO Stefan Thumann told private broadcaster RTL and magazine Stern in a joint interview that he had received warnings from German officials in late 2025 that Russian intelligence was plotting against his life.

"I'm fighting a duel with the Russian military intelligence service for my life," he said. 

"Constantly changing locations, 24-hour personal security, my private life has ceased to exist," he added.

Thumann said he now slept next to his computer and started working as soon as he woke up to distract himself from his personal situation.

In March, prosecutors said they had arrested two so-called disposable agents who were alleged to have spied on Thumann to prepare an assassination attempt.  

Disposable agents is the name given to people with little training who are hired by military intelligence services for small pay to carry out missions.

But RTL cited a German lawmaker who said there were still plans targeting him that were well advanced and involved using a nerve agent.

In 2024, the United States thwarted a Russian plot to assassinate the chief executive of German defense giant Rheinmetall, Armin Papperger, according to a CNN report.
