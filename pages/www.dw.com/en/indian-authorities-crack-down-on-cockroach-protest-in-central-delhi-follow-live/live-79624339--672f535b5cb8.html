Indian police crack down on 'Cockroach' protest in Delhi

Akanksha Saxena | Mahima Kapoor | Dharvi Vaid Dhulia with AFP, AP, ANI, Reuters
Published 10/10/2026Published October 10, 2026last updated 10/10/2026last updated October 10, 2026

Indian authorities have imposed extraordinary security measures to block protesters from joining a demonstration called by the Cockroach Janta Party in New Delhi. The main leaders of the party have been detained too.

What you need to know

  • Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke and other party leaders detained on landing in Delhi
  • 43,000 police and security personnel deployed across Delhi
  • Internet services suspended in central Delhi
  • Massive restrictions in place on metro and railway services
  • CJP called for a rally — that has not been authorized by police — to oppose ongoing revision of voter lists and demand resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar

Here is our rolling coverage of developments from India on Saturday, October 10:

Indian opposition leaders detained amid police crackdown on protests

Akanksha Saxena

Videos released by opposition leaders show that several senior figures were detained in New Delhi on Saturday.

They include Arvind Kejriwal, leader of the Aam Aadmi Party and former chief minister of Delhi.

"It's official... we are living under an absolute dictatorship," Kejriwal wrote on X.

Mamata Banerjee, who lost power as West Bengal's chief minister earlier this year, was also detained.

Pinarayi Vijayan, a communist leader who headed the southern state of Kerala until May, was among those detained. 

Rahul Gandhi, leader of India's opposition Congress party, called the crackdown an act of "cowardice" and described Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Home Minister Amit Shah, right-wing, Hindu nationalist RSS chief Mohan Bhagwat, Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and National Security Adviser Ajit Doval as "the villains of this story" who were "slowly finding the space closing around them. We are closing that space."

Priyanka Gandhi Vadra, Rahul Gandhi's sister and a Congress MP, wrote in a social media post: "India is not a democracy anymore. It is a dictatorship being run by Mr. Narendra Modi and Mr. Amit Shah."

Opposition parties and critics have warned that the electoral-roll revision exercise could strip millions of people of their voting rights and give an advantage to Prime Minister Modi's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).

The Election Commission of India has rejected allegations of wrongdoing, saying the "special intensive revision" aims to remove duplicate and ineligible voters.

The protests echo the CJP's mass mobilization in July, which forced the then education minister to resign over an examination scandal.

This time, demonstrators are demanding the resignation of election chief Gyanesh Kumar.

Police crackdown amid outrage over India voter list revision

Akanksha Saxena

Tens of thousands of security personnel have been deployed and roads barricaded across New Delhi to prevent the latest Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) demonstration.

Police have detained thousands of protesters in a major crackdown. Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke and two other leaders were detained before disembarking from a flight in the capital.

Some protesters who turned back from Jantar Mantar, the designated protest site, have been waiting in different parts of central Delhi, determined to hold their ground.

The CJP has urged people who believe they or someone they know has been unlawfully detained to fill out an online form as soon as possible, saying it is collecting information to support affected volunteers and other individuals.

The protest targeted Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over voter list revisions that critics say favor Prime Minister Narendra Modi's ruling BJP.

'We've been playing a game of cat and mouse with police,' protester tells DW

Dharvi Vaid Dhulia

DW Hindi's Adarsh Sharma, who is on the ground in New Delhi, spoke to protesters as they faced a security crackdown.

He caught up with one protester who managed to avoid detention.

"We were running back and forth. When the police are moving forward, we go back. When they are withdrawing, we are moving forward," the protester told DW. 

The protester described the heavy police presence as a "game of perception."

"Where you have 20 protesters and 10 media persons, there are 100 police personnel standing. It seems like a joke, what they are doing with taxpayers' money," the protester said.

Another protester, Jasmine, described how the morning unfolded for the demonstrators. 

"We have been playing a game of cat and mouse with the police, trying to stop them from picking us up," she said. 

"I don't think we have ever seen something like this happening. Protesting is a fundamental right… it's such a convoluted idea to take permission to protest… you are protesting against a government, will you ask permission from the same government? They are not going to give it," she said.

Indian media houses say their journalists were briefly detained during Delhi protests

Akanksha Saxena

Indian media outlets, including Scroll and Newslaundry, reported that their journalists had been detained by police while covering the 'Cockroach' party protest. The journalists were later released.

Newslaundry reported that police seized its reporters’ phones, while other reports said journalists were physically assaulted.


The Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ), an independent nonprofit organization that promotes press freedom and defends journalists' rights worldwide, has called on Delhi Police to immediately release all detained journalists and media workers.

It has also urged police to return their equipment, investigate reports of physical abuse, and allow the press to cover the ongoing protests in New Delhi and the police response without interference.

In the 2026 World Press Freedom Index published by Reporters Without Borders (RSF), India ranked 157th out of 180 countries, down six places from 2025. Press freedom groups have raised concerns about the use of criminal laws against journalists and the impact of such measures on independent journalism.

Skip next section CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke's grandmother has died — reports

CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke's grandmother has died — reports

Dharvi Vaid Dhulia

Detained Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) founder Abhijeet Dipke's grandmother is reported to have died on Saturday.

Indian media reports said that Dipke's paternal grandmother had been suffering from a prolonged illness and had been unwell in recent days. She died in her native village in Maharashtra, reports said. 

News agency ANI, citing police sources, reported that the Delhi Police had informed Dipke of his grandmother's death. It was now up to the CJP leader to decide whether to continue his protest while under detention or travel back for the funeral rites. 

Dipke was detained aboard his plane after it landed in New Delhi on Saturday morning, ahead of the protest. He was traveling from Mumbai.

CJP appoints interim protest leader to press on with rally

Dharvi Vaid Dhulia

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) has indicated that it will press on with its protest despite its leaders and supporters being detained.

CJP's National Organization In-Charge Ajinkya Shinde will lead the protest at Jantar Mantar until Abhijeet Dipke, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka remain in detention, the group said in a statement on X. 

The CJP also shared a video of Dipke purportedly recorded on Friday night, anticipating a potential detention.

"If you are seeing this video, it means Ashutosh, Saurav and I have been arrested," Dipke says in the video.

"After us, Ajinkya will lead this movement," he says. 

In a separate post, the CJP alleged that its three detained leaders had been taken to an undisclosed location, were being denied access to lawyers and had been cut off from "all contact with others."

Skip next section Supreme Court bar association calls for intervention after lawyers are detained
10/10/2026October 10, 2026

Supreme Court bar association calls for intervention after lawyers are detained

Mahima Kapoor

The formal association of advocates at India's apex court called for urgent intervention by Chief Justice of India (CJI) Surya Kant after several lawyers were detained by Delhi police.

This comes amid a reported crackdown on protesters attempting to reach the national capital, specifically central Delhi, to protest against India's chief election commissioner over alleged misconduct.

The arrested lawyers include those protesting outside the premises of the Supreme Court of India, as well as lawyers inside their chambers, legal news website, Bar and Bench, reported. 

The Supreme Court Bar Association passed a resolution condemning the use of "excessive force against, and detention of, senior and young advocates, particularly women advocates, who were peacefully protesting in the Supreme Court area today."

The advocates' body expressed "grave concern" and requested the CJI to take action to ensure that the "whereabouts and safety of every detained advocate are ascertained."

Skip next section How have India's opposition politicians reacted to the Delhi protest crackdown?
10/10/2026October 10, 2026

How have India's opposition politicians reacted to the Delhi protest crackdown?

Dharvi Vaid Dhulia

Congress MP Shashi Tharoor has slammed the measures taken by Indian authorities to prevent the Cockroach Janta Party's protest from going ahead.

He called out a "war-like deployment" of security personnel and said that the suspension of the internet in a key part of Delhi was among the "most regressive" and "repressive" measures.

"A government confident in the fairness of its elections would welcome questions about them, whereas one that turns its capital into a fortress to avoid them only deepens the very suspicions it seeks to suppress. The government should provide answers, not lathis (sticks); expression, not repression," Tharoor wrote on X.

He also called for the release of the protesters who had been detained.

Tharoor said India's Gen Z "deserves better than an undeclared Emer-GenZ," in a play on words.

Leaders of the CJP on Friday likened the clampdown on their planned protest to a "state of emergency."

A state of emergency was declared in India by former Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in the mid-70s. Emergency powers were invoked by the Congress leader for 21 months, during which civil liberties were suspended, much of the opposition was jailed and media was censored. 

Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) National Convenor and former Delhi chief minister Arvind Kejriwal also mounted an emergency-related attack against Modi's government. 

"This is worse than the Emergency. It's official.. we are living under an absolute dictatorship ruled by Modi. Arresting cockroaches straight off their flight shows an unprecedented level of fear," he said, referring to the arrests of CJP's three main leaders.

Skip next section Modi's party accuses opposition of attempts to 'incite tension'
10/10/2026October 10, 2026

Modi's party accuses opposition of attempts to 'incite tension'

Mahima Kapoor

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) on Saturday accused opposition leaders including Rahul Gandhi and Arvind Kejriwal of "attempting to incite tensions" in the country.

"The Govt of India will deal strictly with the attempts you are making to incite tension, and BJP workers, remaining constantly among the people, will effectively thwart your designs," BJP President Nitin Nabin told news agencies on Saturday morning.

"Your repeated electoral defeats in various states and the resulting desperation suggest that, in some way, you do not wish to see this country move forward peacefully. However, I warn you today that the BJP and the patriotic citizens of this country will give you a fitting and stern response," he said. 

Nabin suggested that "foreign forces" were behind the opposition leaders and that the protests were an attempt to "destabilize" India. BJP leaders have made similar claims before without offering proof.

"I issue a clear warning to you today: the foreign forces upon which you rely for your politics up for sale—and the alliances with such forces through which you seek to destabilize India—will never allow your nefarious intentions to succeed," he said.

What to know if you're joining us now

Mahima Kapoor

Here's the situation right now:

  • The leaders of the Cockroach Janta Party, which called Saturday's protest, have been detained
  • Police have reportedly detained more than 500 people heading toward Jantar Mantar in anticipation of demonstrations
  • Heavy barricades have been erected across central Delhi and along the national capital's borders to prevent protesters from entering the city

With this, it is unclear at the moment whether the planned protest will be able to take place.

But the CJP isn't the only group protesting against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. 

The left-wing All India Students' Association (AISA) and its breakout star, Neha Bora, have been leading protests in the capital all week.

Also on the ground are the Gandhi siblings, lawmakers Rahul Gandhi and Priyanka Gandhi, who are leading the opposition alliance's protests against the poll body in New Delhi.

Rahul Gandhi is the leader of the opposition in the lower house of the Indian parliament and possibly Prime Minister Modi's biggest challenger in the 2029 elections. According to Indian media reports, Gandhi is scheduled to attend a political meeting in the southern city of Chennai today.

10/10/2026October 10, 2026

Gyanesh Kumar, Modi government 'will go,' says Rahul Gandhi

Dharvi Vaid Dhulia

India's main opposition leader Rahul Gandhi on Saturday reiterated his demand for Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar step down and critized the Modi government.

"Gyanesh Kumar will go. This government will go. Then the repair of democracy will begin," the Congress leader wrote on X as he shared a video of himself addressing INDIA bloc MPs in the Central Hall of Parliament. 

Late on Friday, Gandhi and a delegation of opposition lawmakers met President Droupadi Murmu to submit a four-point memorandum.

The memorandum outlined what Gandhi called Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah's "gross subversion of India's electoral system" and called for the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.
