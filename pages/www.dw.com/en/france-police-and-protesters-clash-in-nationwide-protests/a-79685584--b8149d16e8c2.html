France: Protesters rally nationwide amid rising social polarization

Timothy Jones | Kieran Burke with AFP, Reuters, AP
10/17/2026October 17, 2026

Students, trade unionists and Yellow Vest activists are taking to French streets to protest high prices and inequality. Police and protesters have already clashed in Paris and Marseille.

There have been clashes between police and protesters amid mass rallies  across France on Saturday, over high prices and social inequalities and the state of the education system.

Police arrest hundreds nationwide

The Interior Ministry said that some 272 people had been arrested by mid-afternoon following violent incidents in Paris, Marseilles, Rennes, Toulouse and Angers.

"Such actions include attempted looting, confrontations with law enforcement, the setting of fires to street furniture and the targeting of seats of power and media outlets," the ministry saidand blamed the unrest on what it described ​as the "ultra-left."

France protests escalate as unrest spreads across cities

In Paris and Marseille, police have fired tear gas at protesters during clashes.

AFP reported that the windows of the offices of French broadcasters CNews and Europe 1 in western Paris were broken by protesters, and journalists reported seeing several parked cars torched.

French President Emmanuel Macron condemned the violence in a post on X and warned: "Those who have broken, attacked the prefectures as well as businesses, looted even our town halls, will be prosecuted and punished." 

Buildings set alight amid unrest

In Lyon, situated in central France, the city's tourism office was set alight with flames and black smoke seen billowing from the building.

In the city of Limoges, the town hall was also set alight as unrest broke out. The blaze spread from a wing of the building and eventually reached the roof where it continued burning as crowds stood watching.

Firefighters were then seen attmepting to douse the flames.

Meanwhile in Lille, protesters smashed their way into the town hall of France's northern city of Lille and vandalised the premises before leaving, according to an AFP photographer.

Riot police within minutes confirmed that all the protesters had left. 

The protests come on the heels of an initial wave of demonstrations that began last month over the situation at schools in the country, with pupils complaining about teacher absences and run-down facilities.

Macron had called "for calm and respect" ahead of the protests, saying that the right to protest went hand in hand with "respect for people and property."

France students demand better schools

What do we know about the protests in France?

More than 600 demonstrations were planned across France, according to protest organizers.

Interior Minister Laurent Nunez told France 2 television that "a pretty big turnout" was expected, while a police source told the French AFP news agency that authorities estimate nearly 200,000 people will attend.

However, the Paris demonstrators faced restrictions, with police banning rallies around the Elysee presidential palace, the prime minister's office and the Champs-Elysee avenue, along with other key locations.

A march through the center of the city has also been banned.

The main Paris demonstration began at 2 p.m. (1200 GMT) at the Place de la Republique..

Reports began emerging late on Saturday morning of riot police clashing with protesters in the Saint-Denis area on the outskirts of the capital, as well as near the Gare du Nord train station, among other areas.

Who is taking part in the protests?

Students have been joined by trade unionists and remnants of the Yellow Vests ("Gilets jaunes") movement, which staged mass weekly protests from November 2018 to June 2020, initially against rising fuel prices. They later morphed into protests against the high cost of living and economic inequality in general.

The student protests over the past weeks have been backed by Jean-Luc Melanchon, the leader of the hard-left France Unbowed ("La France insoumise", or LFI), who marched in one demonstration on Tuesday.

The government has blamed the LFI for pouring fuel on the fire of widespread public discontent, an allegation the party has denied.
