Jordan bans Telegram over 'extremism' and 'radicalization' concerns

Sertac Comak with Reuters, AFP
11/16/2026November 16, 2026

Jordan has imposed a ban on Telegram over a deadly church attack which left seven people dead. Iraq later followed with a partial ban to compel the company to review its content.

Jordan on Monday announced a ban on instant messaging service Telegram after a deadly attack on a church last month.

Jordan's ‌Public ⁠Security Directorate said the move aimed to protect the society from "the attempts to spread extremism and radicalisation."

All of which pose a "threat to societal security and national interests," it said.

In December 2022, Jordan also banned TikTok over videos authorities claimed "promoted killing and chaos."

What prompted the ban?

The ban on Telegram comes after a shooting on October 23 at a Greek Orthodox church in the southern Karak governate which left seven people dead and another 34 wounded.

Jordan, in retaliation, organised airstrikes on neighbouring Syria and said it had killed 20 so-called "Islamic State" militants. The government also acknowledged that a number of civilians had been killed in the strikes.

A Jordanian court sentenced three men to death by hanging on Sunday in connection with the attack. Authorities identified them as "followers of a hardline Islamist ideology."

Attacks on Christian minorities are very rare in Jordan, a relatively stable Middle Eastern state and close ally of the United States and which maintains diplomatic and military relations with neighboring Israel.

Christians make up around 3% of Jordan's population of 10 million. The Levantine country is home to one of the oldest Christian communities in the world.

Iraq gave the company 30 days

The media and communications authority in Iraq said on Monday the ban was to pressure Telegram to review Iraqi content and "take measures to prevent violations."

"The operating company ​failed to take the necessary measures to address non-compliant content published on the platform and did not adhere to Iraqi laws," the authority said in a statement.

It gave Telegram 30 days ⁠to ​comply, with no indication of what action would follow.

Telegram and 'IS'

Over the years, so-called "Islamic State" militants have used Telegram for propaganda and communication purposes.

The Russian-born billionaire Pavel Durov, now based in Dubai, built the Telegram messaging app with his brother Nikolai and launched it in 2013.

In July, Russia ‌said it had charged him with "aiding terrorist activities," accusing the platform of hosting tools used to coordinate deadly attacks and sabotage across Russia. 

Durov rejects the allegations, arguing that Telegram has gone "beyond its obligations in moderating content and assisting authorities in tackling crime." 

He describes himself as a libertarian and has repeatedly clashed with governments seeking greater access to the platform.
