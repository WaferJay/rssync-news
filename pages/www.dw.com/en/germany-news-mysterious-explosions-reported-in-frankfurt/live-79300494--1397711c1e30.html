Klingbeil visits struggling VW, calls for tougher EU line on Chinese cars

Saim Dušan Inayatullah Editor

German Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil has called on the EU to widen tariffs against Chinese imports to include hybrid cars, saying that Chinese manufacturing subsidies were endangering Volkswagen and others. 

KIingbeil made the call on a visit to VW's Wolfsburg HQ in Lower Saxony; it also coincided with a joint appeal from the heads of Germany's three major carmaking states, Bavaria (BMW, Audi), Baden-Württemberg (Porsche, Mercedes) and Lower Saxony (VW). 

"I am in favor of us standing up against unfair trade practices, and for Germany to adopt a different level of self-confidence and assertiveness towards countries that threaten our industry," Klingbeil, who leads the Social Democrats and is also deputy chancellor, said in Wolfsburg. 

The VW Group is just embarking on a major restructuring and cost-cutting drive amid falling sales figures, planning to cut or otherwise phase out as may as 100,000 jobs in the coming years across its various marques — almost a sixth of its workforce. 

The unpopular restructuring was approved earlier this month despite resistance from trade unions and from Lower Saxony's state government, which is led by Klingbeil's center-left Social Democrats (SPD). Klingbeil said it should be just the beginning of an ongoing transformation. 

He said the EU would have to tighten local content rules and introduce tariffs on Chinese-made plug-in hybrid cars. 

In what seemed a coordinated appeal, the state premiers of Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg and Lower Saxony, each representing a different German political party, appealed to the federal government in Berlin and authorities in Brussels to act with haste to protect the car industry. 

"Without a combination of competitive conditions in the domestic market, effective protective instruments and fair trade within the bounds of the WTO, the car industry is threatened with irreversible damage," state premiers Markus Söder (Bavaria), Olaf Lies (Lower Saxony) and Cem Özdemir (Baden-Württemberg) wrote.

Söder belongs to the conservative Christian Social Union (CSU), Lies is in the SPD and Özdemir belongs to the environmentalist Greens.

The three premiers called for tariffs on Chinese plug-in hybrids, to be modeled on existing fees imposted on all-electric cars from China. 

In 2025 alone, the German car industry shed more than 50,000 jobs.

German court holds Meta held liable for fake ads

A German court on Thursday ruled that Facebook parent company Meta is liable for fake advertising on its social media platforms.

The case involved ads using the logo of a financial advice website, alongside photos of the website's founder, to promote fraudulent investment schemes.

The Frankfurt regional court found that Meta was liable, citing a European Court of Justice (ECJ) decision ruling a service provider is liable for content that it exercises control over.

Meta was ordered to cease the publication and distribution of the fake ads, compensate the claimant for any damages, and disclose the revenue generated.

Meta had argued that it had no knowledge of the fake ads, but the Frankfurt court said it was up to the social media giant to decide how ads are published on its platforms.

The decision can be appealed, and a spokesperson for Meta said the company was considering further legal steps. Meta said it had taken several measures in the case, including removing the content after it was reported.

Heat and drought set to wither Germany's 2026 potato harvest

Extreme heat and drought this summer will lead to a weak potato harvest in northwestern Europe this year, according to an analysis released on Thursday by the UK-based Energy and Climate Intelligence Unit (ECIU).

In Germany, France, Belgium and the Netherlands, a combined shortage of 3,1 million tons compared to the five-year average harvest is expected.

The data shows the weak harvest will hit Germany particularly hard, with the country's share of the total expected potato harvest shortage coming in at 1,13 million tons.

These figures do not yet represent a final harvest report, but are an estimate.

"As fall begins, the effects of the record-breaking summer heat and drought on food and agriculture are becoming clear," said ECIU analyst Tom Lancaster.

Potatoes are among the crops in Europe that require the most water.

The analysis also reports a significant increase in prices. In Germany, spuds served at dinner are set to cost up to €280 per metric ton, compared to around €150 last year.

The expected 2026 harvest shortfall comes after oversupply in 2025 flooded the market.

Germany backs social media ban for under-13s

Emilio Reynoso

Karin Prien,Germany's education minister, backed plans to restrict social media use among young people and children. "Any system will have to comply with data protection rules and minimize the amount of information that is collected about the user, without age-verification data being transmitted online. However, the primary responsibility for protecting minors lies with the social media platforms themselves," Prien told German public broadcaster ZDF on Thursday.

In addition to requesting age checks, Prien said media education and providing more information on the issue to parents are key. On Wednesday, the president of the European Commission,Ursula von der Leyen, proposed that children under the age of 13 be banned from accessing social media platforms such asTikTok, Snapchat, and Instagram. They would be able to use "mini-accounts" controlled and supervised by parents, with a maximum usage of one hour per day, and open a separate account from the age of 15.

Changes to social media platforms

Prien said the restrictions should be part of a broader, more comprehensive strategy. She also called for a rule under which children under the age of three have no screen time or access to digital services. The education minister announced plans as early as October for a German interim solution while rules are consolidated at the EU level. Under the EU proposals, social media platforms would be required to introduce safer designs, including measures to prevent endless scrolling and videos from playing automatically. The plans also include limits on screen time and other measures aimed at protecting young people.

Authorities advise mosquito protection amid malaria cases near Frankfurt Airport

Health authorities in Frankfurt are advising residents to consider taking precautions against mosquitoes after several recent malaria cases in the region believed to have been caused by the insects having stowed away on aircraft.

This week, two people living in the vicinity of Frankfurt Airport were reported to have contracted malaria.

In August,  two Frankfurt Airport employees died after contracting malaria in an outbreak believed to have been caused by infected mosquitoes brought into Germany on an aircraft. Four other workers were infected, but recovered without complications.

The cases are part of a rare outbreak of so-called airport malaria, with the six employees infected despite not having traveled to a malaria-risk country. Authorities have not shared details about the two cases this week.

According to Germany's Robert Koch health institute, cases of airport malaria are extremely rare.

Data cited by DPA news agency shows that between 1969 and 2024, only 145 cases of locally contracted malaria were reported in Europe. At least 105 of those cases involved mosquitoes arriving on planes.

Most of these cases were in France, with only 9 reported in Germany in this time frame, making the 2026 outbreak a statistical outlier.

Malaria is native to tropical environments, and the vast majority of cases in Europe occur in summer.

Merz to campaign in Berlin ahead of critical state election

Chancellor Friedrich Merz will appear on Thursday at joint campaign event with lead Christian Democratic Union (CDU) candidate Stefan Evers, ahead of the Berlin House of Representatives election on Sunday.

Merz is facing pressure for the CDU to perform in Berlin after the CDU's state election drubbing earlier this month in Saxony-Anhalt.

On Wednesday, all eight Christian Democratic Union (CDU) state premiers  publicly backed Merz amid growing unease over his leadership.

In Berlin, only a few percentage points separate the top four parties: the conservative CDU, the Left Party, the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD), and the environmentalist Greens.

The CDU's lead candidate, state finance minister Stefan Evers, has managed to keep the conservatives' chances afloat, with the party is polling around 20%, narrowly behind The Left and just ahead of the AfD.

A state election in nearby Mecklenburg- West Pomerania will also take place Sunday, with polling showing the CDU barely hanging on at around 7%, which is dangerously close to the 5% threshold needed to win seats in parliament.

Although the CDU has not traditionally performed well in the northeast German state, a 7% result at the polls would be the worst CDU election performance since World War II.

Diesel prices in Germany hit record high

Figures from the ADAC motoring organization released on Thursday show German diesel prices have surged to a new record, reaching an average price per liter of €2.45 ($2.81).

For US readers, that is equivalent to over $10.60 per gallon. 

Since the Iran war started, fuel prices in Germany have surged. On Wednesday, the ADAC reported the price of ordinary gasoline at another record high of  €2.314 ($2.65) per liter on average.

Measures to provide price relief in the spring expired in June, but German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said on Tuesday that the government would "react to price gouging." 

No new measures have yet been announced. Coalition partners the Social Democrats (SPD), have called for a windfall tax on oil companies, an idea Merz has rejected.

Mysterious explosions reported in Frankfurt

Residents in two Frankfurt neighborhoods were shaken from sleep early Thursday morning by mysterious explosions.

Police said explosives were set off shortly after 4 a.m. in front of a cafe in the city's "Nordend" neighborhood, and a few minutes later in front of a kiosk in the city center. 

No injuries were reported. Images of the cafe showed a shattered glass facade and shards blown out onto the street. 

"We have officers on the scene," a police spokeswoman told Germany's DPA news agency. "We're working hard on it," she added, referring to the ongoing investigation.

Police have not released any information about a motive or possible suspects.

In the past few days, there had been two explosions in Frankfurt and one in nearby Offenbach. Both caused minor damage and no injuries.  

Early Monday morning, there was an explosion at an apartment building in Frankfurt-Bockenheim.

Early Sunday morning, there were also explosions in Frankfurt's Sachsenhausen district and in Offenbach. A bar, a cafe, and a newsstand were damaged in the incidents.

Following the explosion in Sachsenhausen, a 15-year-old suspect was arrested and is currently in pretrial detention.

This week's blasts come after repeated explosions in the Rhine-Main region over the past weeks and months.

Police have attributed several of these incidents to organized crime and the phenomenon of "violence as a service," in which teenagers and young adults are specifically recruited to carry out these acts, local public broadcaster HR reported. 

Authorities have not said whether they consider Thursday morning's explosions to be one of these cases.
