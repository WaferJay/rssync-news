Serbia's Student List runs chemistry professor for president

Darko Janjevic
11/27/2026November 27, 2026

After ousting strongman Aleksandar Vucic, Serbia's student movement has now nominated academic Radomir Saicic for the upcoming presidential vote. Saicic accused the "outgoing regime" of trying to collapse the state.

A long-time supporter of the Serbian student movement, chemistry professor and researcher Radomir Saicic, is set to run for president on behalf of the Student List in late December.

Saicic accepted the nomination on Friday, a day after Serbia's new parliament scheduled the ballot for December 27.

We were there when Serbia's student movement toppled the government

In his acceptance speech, the Belgrade University lecturer said state institutions, including its president, needed to "win back public trust."

"It's time to restore dignity to the office of Serbian president," he said.

"Once we can agree with each other about things that matter to all of us despite our differences, Serbia will have the strength to decide its future on its own, and to have its voice heard and respected in the world," he added.

Vucic barred from running again

The 65-year-old is one of the lawmakers in the new assembly, which was formed in the wake of the Student List ousting the regime of Serbia's longtime strongman ruler Aleksandar Vucic at the parliamentary polls last month.

Lessons from Serbia: How students defeated an authoritarian leader

Vucic is banned by the constitution from running for president after winning two terms. He has hinted at his party boycotting the presidential vote.

While the office of the Serbian president is officially far less powerful that the office of Serbian prime minister, Vucic managed to organize his rule around himself during his time as prime minister and then hold on to levers of power while serving as president.

Saicic sees Kosovo as 'occupied territory'

On Friday, Saicic accused the "outgoing regime" of "still trying to collapse our state."

"This past decade was marred by the breakdown of the rule of law, corruption, and hate speech," he said.

Saicic, a member of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Art, is seen by many as a moderate nationalist and a traditionalist.

He has notably described the neigboring, Albanian-dominated Kosovo, as "temporarily occupied territory," keeping in line with Belgrade's long-running policy of treating Kosovo as a breakaway province.

The scandals that rocked Serbia and are shaping the election

The presidential candidate has also urged a "productive relationship" with Russia, pointing to Serbia's energy needs.

Saicic pledges humility with Bible quote

While seemingly reticent on the topic of the European Union, Saicic told reporters on Friday that he has a "fundamentally positive stance toward Europe and European values," pointing to examples including environment protection, academic education, the rule of law, science and technological progress.

"All of these things we should develop as much as possible, not for the sake of meeting some formal criteria, but to actually be what we are supposed to be," he said.

Saicic has sought to present himself as a devout Orthodox Christian while simultaneously criticizing the Serbian church leaders for aligning with the Vucic regime.

In his Friday speech, Saicic quoted the Bible, saying that "whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant."
