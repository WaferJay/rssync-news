German universities draw in largest number of foreign students in EU

Mark Hallam

New figures from the EU statistics agency show that just over one quarter of all students from abroad in various EU member states were enrolled in tertiary education in Germany. 

Of a total of roughly 1.65 million foreign students in 2024, around 1.1 million of them were from outside the EU and the rest were moving within the EU, 438,260 were in Germany. 

France was next in line by sheer numbers with almost 285,000 foreign students, and the much less populous Netherlands was third with over 176,000. Germany, France and the Netherlands combined accounted for more than half the bloc-wide total. 

The Netherlands, unlike France and Germany, was also one of the countries with the highest proportion of foreign students in tertiary education. Even excluding doctoral-level students, around 18% of students in the Netherlands hailed from abroad. 

Only Luxembourg, at 51.5%, Malta (35.6%), Cyprus (21.6%) and Austria (20.8%)  had a higher share among full EU members, though Switzerland's ratio also stood at roughly 20%. 

Greece (2.6%), Croatia (3.5%) and Spain (4.6'%) had some of the lowest percentages of foreign students, with the EU average at 8.7%.

UN rights chief voices concerns over Rwanda deportation plans

Timothy Jones

The UN's high commissioner for human rights, Volker Türk, has joined the chorus of those voicing doubts about a plan by five EU countries, including Germany, to send rejected asylum seekers ​to a deportation hub ​in ‌Rwanda.

Türk said he had not yet ‌seen the draft proposal ‌but that ​his office was "concerned about the human rights risks raised by ​the proposed transfer of people from five ⁠European Union ​countries to ​Rwanda, where they have no ​genuine connection and ‌which has its own set ​of ⁠human rights challenges."

Instead, he called ⁠for ​a "comprehensive approach to migration governance anchored in human rights."

Lufthansa and VC pilots' union negotiations end without breakthrough

Mark Hallam

Mediation between German flagship carrier Lufthansa and the Vereinigung Cockpit (VC) pilots' trade union ended on Tuesday without a clear breakthrough. 

The airline is trying to cut costs while the union is trying to optimize pay and conditions for members in a dispute dating back to September 2025 that led to several disruptions earlier in the year. 

VC said that it favored a package resulting from the mediation, which it said Lufthansa had rejected. VC said it was giving the airline until November 23 to reconsider its position. 

"The ball is now in the employer's court," VC said in a call with media. It did not specify what action it would take after the deadline but did say it considered its commitment not to strike during the arbitration process to be satisfied. 

Lufthansa, meanwhile, said it had submitted a proposal to VC for up to 1,000 new cockpit hires by 2033, up to 150 additional captain promotions each year, a one-off payment for 2027 and 2028 followed by salary increases from 2029 onwards. 

VC said it would be willing to make further concessions on pay and conditions if Lufthansa would respond with job guarantees.

The airline also said arbitrators had indicated a willingness to continue negotiations if the latest round proved fruitless — though it wasn't clear whether that also applied to each side of the labor dispute.

Berlin bans association accused of supporting Hezbollah

Mark Hallam

The government of the city-state of Berlin has banned an association accused of supporting the Lebanese group Hezbollah, which has been deemed a terrorist organization and outlawed in Germany since 2020. 

Police in Berlin searched a total of 28 properties on Tuesday, including premises belonging to the affected groups and the homes of select members.

The ban affects El-Irschad e.V. in the Neukölln district and three subsidiary groups: Al-Qaem e.V., Al-Thaqalyn e.V., and Qalam Bildungszentrum UG. 

The city's senator, or Cabinet minister, for interior affairs, Iris Spranger of the Social Democrats, said the group was "directed against the notions of understanding between peoples," and that its "purpose and activities" contravened criminal laws in Berlin. 

The ban prohibits the public display or distribution of the organizations' symbols and logos. Their websites have been shut down and their assets seized.

Spranger said the group had given Hezbollah a platform to spread propaganda among Neukölln's Shiite community, using youth activities and a mosque to do so.

Rwanda migrant deportation hub scheme condemned by rights groups

Timothy Jones

A number of human rights groups have spoken out after Germany, along with Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands, announced plans to set up a hub for rejected migrants in Rwanda.

The deportation center would be the first such facility outside the European Union.

The Germany-based refugee advocacy group Pro Asyl said on Tuesday that the plans would end in a "shipwreck."

Pro Asyl managing director Karl Kopp said that even rejected asylum seekers had rights.

"Rwanda is not a model democracy. Opposition figures there are arrested and sometimes tortured," he said.

"If those affected need help, we will stand at their side with legal support."

In a statement issued after the announcement of the plans, 45 NGOs from 15 countries also condemned the move.

"These plans allow people without a right to stay in the EU to be sent to countries with which they have no connection at all," the statement said.

"These policies shift people, and responsibility for their safety and rights, out of public sight and beyond scrutiny," it said.

Criticism from within the coalition

The junior partner in Germany's coalition, the center-left Social Democrats, has also maintained its criticism of the plans, which are heavily supported by Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt of the Bavarian Christian Social Union (CSU), the sister party of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democrats (CDU). 

"Dobrindt's plans are short-sighted with regard to foreign policy and harm Germany's credibility," SPD foreign policy spokesman Adid Ahmetovic told the Reuters news agency.

"Rwanda violates the sovereignty of its neighbors and supports the M23 rebels in eastern [Democratic Republic of] Congo," he said.

"Such a regime must not receive any political concessions just because it is to take in refugees from Germany," he said, adding: "Human rights are not bargaining chips for deportation deals." 

Rwanda deportation hub planned for 2027

The announcement of the plans comes after an EU reform of its migration policy this year granted broader detention powers and allowed for the creation of return hubs. 

According to Austrian Chancellor Christian Stocker, the aim is for the first center to be set up in Rwanda next year.

 Diplomatic sources say the Rwanda hub will be initially run as a pilot with some 100 people, 20 from each of the five nations.

Germany funding $300 million more US arms supplies for Ukraine — Pistorius

Timothy Jones

Germany will be purchasing $300 million (€273 million) of US arms supplies to give to Ukraine as it fights against the Russian invasion,  German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius announced on Tuesday.

He said that Berlin was making a fifth contribution to the PURL (Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List) mechanism coordinated by the US and NATO, under which European countries finance the purchase of US weapons for Ukraine.

Most of the new funds will go to buy guided missiles for Patriot air defense systems, Pistorius said in remarks made on the sidelines of a meeting with his EU counterparts in Brussels.

He said Berlin was also planning to provide Kyiv with IRIS-T air defense systems, as previously announced, as well as new radars to improve air surveillance, ammunition for F-16 fighter jets, and drones.

He also said that Germany was preparing to make IRIS-T missiles compatible with NASAMS air defense systems being supplied to Ukraine by Norway.

Pistorius stressed that supporting Ukraine, although an act of solidarity, was "not a charity event," as Russian aggression posed an associated threat to Western Europe.

He is to host a meeting alongside his British counterpart Wes Streeting later on Tuesday that aims to drum up further military support for Kyiv.

Skip next section Wadephul wants closer ties with Azerbaijan, Armenia to counter Russia
11/17/2026November 17, 2026

Wadephul wants closer ties with Azerbaijan, Armenia to counter Russia

Timothy Jones

Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul has said he would like Germany to expand its relations with the former Soviet republics of Azerbaijan and Armenia as a counterweight to an ongoing bid by Russia to maintain its influence in the region.

"In the South Caucasus too, we are seeing Russia try to retain its influence at the expense of the people and their future," Wadephul on Tuesday before setting off on his first visit to the two countries as foreign minister.

He said Russian President Vladimir Putin was opposed to countries deciding their own destiny under a European order of sovereign states, making it important for the two Caucasian nations to retain their self-determination over their futures.

Wadephul also highlighted the South Caucasus's role as an interface between Europe and Asia, which he said meant the region had great potential for trade, energy partnerships and new transport links.

German foreign minister in the Caucasus

Germany's top diplomat is first to visit the Azerbaijani capital, Baku, where he is set to meet the country's authoritarian head of state, Ilham Aliyev, and Foreign Minister Ceyhun Bayramov.

He is scheduled to travel on to Armenia on Wednesday.

In August 2025, the two countries signed a joint declaration on a US-brokered peace treaty ending a decadeslong conflict over the Nagorno-Karabakh region.

This came after Azerbaijan captured the region in 2023 in a short but heavy offensive, with most of the region's population — more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians — forced to flee.

Skip next section Deutsche Bank shares plummet after reports on incriminating documents leak
11/17/2026November 17, 2026

Deutsche Bank shares plummet after reports on incriminating documents leak

Timothy Jones

Shares in Deutsche Bank took a nosedive on Tuesday after media across the world published reports with information gleaned from millions of leaked documents that expose dubious past business practices at the lender.

The documents, which were shared by unidentified whistleblowers, throw light on the banking giant's activities over the past 20 years, which include dealings with Russian oligarchs and the late convicted US sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

The name Donald Trump also appears in the documents from the time before he became US president.

The leaked documents, which include emails, have cast doubt on the success of chief executive Christian Sewing's efforts to turn around the institution after a series of scandals.

Among other things, they indicate that Sewing himself was involved in a working group that  sought to expand the bank's Russian operations shortly before Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, despite warnings about regulatory and money-laundering risks.

The investigation, called "Inside Deutsche Bank", was conducted by over 200 journalists from a media consortium that included The Financial Times, Der Spiegel, Le Monde, the New York Times, the German public broadcaster ZDF and the Austrian daily Der Standard.

Deutsche Bank has not disputed the authenticity of the data, though it refers to the documents as "purported internal documents."

It also said that many of the incidents referred to in them belonged to a distant past and that it is now "a different bank."

Number of start-ups set to reach highest level since 2015

Timothy Jones

Some 286,000 new businesses are expected to be founded in 2026, a rise of 3.4% compared with last year and the highest level of start-ups since 2015, according to the National Association of German Cooperative Banks (BVR).

The BVR said the boom in start-ups could be partly attributed to the fact that developments in artificial intelligence (AI) and a difficult situation on the labor market were making self-employment more profitable.

However, the association warned that the increase was fragile, as it was largely driven by government support and exports rather that the private investment needed for more sustained growth.

In July, the German government adopted a start-up strategy that aimed to cut bureaucracy and provide support for future technologies, thus creating favorable conditions for young entrepreneurs.

The BVR also cautioned that the number of people in the 30-40 age group — those most likely to start a business — was falling, meaning that the surge will probably not last.

The association also said that even though there was a rise in AI-related start-ups in 2026, Europe needed to do more for AI development to keep up with the US and China.
