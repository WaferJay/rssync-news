Brussels' most anticipated new museum opens its doors

Elizabeth Grenier
11/27/2026November 27, 2026

A major addition to Europe's cultural scene: Kanal-Centre Pompidou, the new contemporary art museum in a former Citroen factory complex, is celebrating its opening with a free, nonstop weekend program.

Brussels is best known as the de facto capital of the European Union. But beyond its role as a political hub, it also boasts a thriving contemporary art scene.

Yet until now, the Belgian city didn't have a major museum of modern and contemporary art on the scale of Paris' Pompidou or London's Tate Modern. But this will now change with the opening of Kanal-Centre Pompidou on November 28.

The museum aims to "make Brussels a world-class cultural destination," on par with the Guggenheim in Bilbao or the MoMA in New York, Boris Dillies, the head of the regional government, told press agency AFP.

Behind this new museum is an ambitious architectural project involving the transformation of a vast industrial landmark into a multidisciplinary cultural institution.

A modern art museum inside a car factory

Instead of building the new museum from scratch, Kanal was designed to occupy the former Citroen garage and factory complex, built in 1933-34 beside the Brussels canal.

Originally standing as a symbol of modernity and industrial progress, the factory was a space where cars were serviced and assembled, as well as displayed. The former showroom has been transformed into as a glass-and-steel space that rises 21 meters (some 70 feet) high.

Rather than erasing the building's industrial past, the project's architects — noAarchitecten, EM2N and Sergison Bates, working together as Atelier Kanal — decided to celebrate its original structures. Vast concrete volumes and industrial details remain visible, creating a dialogue between the site's history and its new cultural purpose.

Like London's Tate Modern, which transformed a power station into a museum, Kanal shows how industrial architecture can gain a second life without losing its identity.

"Kanal reveals how architecture can reinvent the museum typology, not only as a place for art, but as a stage for the city and its citizens," Atelier Kanal said in a statement, adding that their concept for the former Citroen factory ensured that the space would be "renewed without being erased, made precise without being overdetermined and open to continued transformation."

Covering around 40,000 square meters (430,550 square feet), the complex is one of the largest cultural buildings in Belgium.

Kanal, Pompidou launch 5-year partnership

Kanal has established a partnership agreement with the Centre Pompidou in Paris that has guided both the international outlook of the new museum, as well as its intention to remain rooted in Brussels' local culture.

"A place for all people" was the founding vision of the Centre Pompidou, articulated by its architects Richard Rogers and Renzo Piano as they designed the iconic institution in the 1970s.

Kanal's collaboration with the Centre Pompidou, which runs until 2031, gives the Brussels museum access to one of the world's greatest collections of modern and contemporary art. Visitors will be able to see works that normally reside in Paris, while also encountering pieces from Belgian collections and newly commissioned projects.

The Brussels museum has already announced some of the upcoming exhibitions that will be drawing from the Pompidou's collection during their five-year partnership: two photography exhibitions focusing on Robert Frank and Brassaï, as well as art exhibitions focusing on iconic figures of modernism: Robert and Sonia Delaunay, Georges Braque, Pablo Picasso and Henri Matisse.

The new institution, however, emphasizes that it's not a branch of the Pompidou. The partnership is rather envisioned as a dialogue between two museums with different perspectives and histories.

The Centre Pompidou itself has been closed for major renovations since September 2025, and is scheduled to reopen in 2030.

10 opening exhibitions

The headline exhibition, "A truly immense journey," brings together more than 350 works from the collections of Centre Pompidou, Kanal and numerous Belgian lenders, and will feature modern icons including Lygia Clark, Sonia Delaunay, Alberto Giacometti, Natalia Goncharova, Wifredo Lam and Henri Matisse.

Along with this show, nine other exhibitions fill the building.

One of them, "An infinite woman," focuses on how mass-produced image of a Mangbetu woman long served as a colonial symbol and how artists of African descent have reappropriated and subverted this imagery.

Another one, "Right to the City — Right to the Future," explores the architectural transformation of the Brussels' Quartier Nord, which was historically a working-class neighborhood that was largely cleared in the 1960s to become a high-rise business district — displacing over 15,000 residents. 

Museum as a third space

Alongside modern and contemporary art, Kanal is dedicated to architecture, landscape and the performing arts.

This reflects a broader shift in the role of museums, which are increasingly expected to be places where people gather, exchange ideas and engage with contemporary issues rather than simply admire objects behind glass.

The opening program alone demonstrates the museum's ambitions, offering a 36-hour nonstop program of live performances, opening rituals, concerts, DJ sets, films, installations and workshops for children and families, with events open to everyone.

The exhibitions can also be visited for free during the inaugural weekend. Visitors, however, need to have previously booked a time slot; all 30,000 tickets were snapped up within 24 hours of their release.
