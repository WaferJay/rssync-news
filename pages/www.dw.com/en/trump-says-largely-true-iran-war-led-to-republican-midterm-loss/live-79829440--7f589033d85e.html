Democrats narrowly ahead in Mississippi, Alaska as counts continue

Karl Sexton

While the outcome of Tuesday's midterms is clear, two Senate races are still undecided.

In the conservative Deep South state of Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom was leading Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith by fewer than 1,200 votes with 96% of ballots counted, according to AP data.

At a press conference in Jackson on Wednesday morning, Colom thanked voters for their support and said he was "very confident" of delivering what would be a historic win.

If Colom can hold on to his narrow lead, he would become the first Democratic Senator from Mississippi since 1982. 

Colom would also become the first African American from the state to serve as Senator since Blanche Bruce, who was born into slavery. Bruce represented Mississippi in the Senate from 1875 to 1881.

Mississippi's result isn't expected for days, as thousands of uncounted ballots still need to be verified for voter eligibility, according to local media.

In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola was leading Republican Dan Sullivan by just 637 votes with 75% of ballots counted on Wednesday night, according to the Associated Press.

That race has been overshadowed by allegations that aides to Peltola recruited another man called Dan Sullivan in a bid to confuse voters and siphon votes from the Republican candidate.

The other Dan Sullivan had secured 3,600 votes by Wednesday night, considerably more than the 637 votes currently splitting the two frontrunners, AP results show.

Peltola has insisted she "knew nothing about any other candidates on the ballot" except for herself and the incumbent. 

"I am not interested in tricking voters. I know I can beat Dan Sullivan," she said on October 29, the week before the midterms.

Because Alaska uses a ranked-choice system and thousands of early and absentee votes still have to be counted, the result of that Senate race might not be known for weeks.

Trump backs Hegseth despite midterm losses tied to Iran war

Karl Sexton

US President Donald Trump has backed his Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, despite the Iran war being identified as a key factor behind the Republican Party's heavy defeat in Tuesday's midterms.

The US president on Wednesday said that he was not considering firing Hegseth, who has also used the title Secretary of War since Trump rebranded the Pentagon, telling reporters: "I think he's doing a very good job."

Hegseth has championed the US-Israeli campaign against Iran and has clashed with Democratic lawmakers during tense congressional hearings over the US military's budget.

The war has cost US taxpayers tens of billions of dollars and caused fuel prices to spike, issues that proved to be significant liabilities for the Republican Party in the midterms.

Earlier on Wednesday, Trump said it was "largely true" that the Iran war had led to the Republicans' trouncing at the ballot box, while Vice President JD Vance also said it had cost the party and publicly questioned whether the conflict was worth it.

Zelenskyy hopes Democratic gains will translate to more US support for Ukraine

Karl Sexton

Ukrainian President ​Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said he hopes Democratic gains in the US midterms will help boost American support for Ukraine.

Under Donald Trump's leadership, US support for Ukraine in its defense against Russia's full-scale invasion has wavered, while Trump has repeatedly blamed Zelenskyy for prolonging the conflict.

The US leader has also been accused of being too soft on Russia and its president Vladimir Putin.

In an interview on Wednesday with German public broadcaster ZDF, Zelenskyy said he hopes Kyiv will benefit from the Democratic Party winning control of both chambers of Congress.

"I am also ‌grateful to the ⁠Republicans, ⁠but the Democrats speak very clearly about supporting Ukraine and providing assistance to Ukraine," Zelenskyy said in comments provided in German before the interview was aired.

"They speak about renewing the ​connection between the United States and Europe... Therefore, there is hope that a balance of power will emerge ​and that we will receive more support," the Ukrainian president added.

Trump says Iran war impacted midterm outcome

Wesley Dockery

Donald Trump spoke about the outcome of the midterm elections to reporters at the construction site of the White House ballroom.

He hailed the ballroom, which is being built after the East Wing of the White House was demolished, as a "structure the likes of which has never been seen anywhere."   

During the remarks, Trump was asked about Vice President JD Vance's post which blamed the Iran war for the midterm losses to the Democrats. 

"I think it's largely true because it drove the gasoline up a little bit short term," Trump said. "But at the same time, if I had to do it again, I'd have to do it again. Because long term, it's the only answer. You can't let Iran have a nuclear weapon." 

The president also ruled out firing Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth, who has guided efforts on the Iran war, saying "I think he's doing a very good job."    

In regard to the Democrats, Trump said he was willing to work with the opposition. However, he warned the Democrats against impeachment. 

"I think it would destroy the Democrat Party if they did that," the president said, while arguing there is "no reason" for him to be impeached. 

Trump was impeached by the House two times during his first term, but was never convicted and removed by the Senate.

Merz says Trump will continue to bypass Congress

Wesley Dockery

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he believes Trump will continue to bypass the US House and Senate in his last two years by issuing executive orders.  

"Trump has, for the past two years, largely governed without consulting Congress. Why should things be any different now?" Merz said during an economic forum of the center-left Social Democratic Party (SPD). Merz's center-right Christian Democrats are in a governing coalition with the SPD.   

The German chancellor said he wasn't so "excited" about the Democratic victory in the midterms. 

"What will change is budget planning — and of course planning for the armed forces as well," Merz continued. 

The US Constitution grants the Congress the power of the purse. Congress can also influence military factors such as troop levels in Europe.

US VP Vance: 'The reason we lost is the war'

Wesley Dockery

US Vice President JD Vance, in a long post on X, said many voters who backed Trump in the 2024 presidential election showed up to the polls and cast ballots for the Democrats out of anger. 

"The reason we lost is the war," Vance said, referring to the US-Israel war on Iran. 

"If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so," Vance said. "Argue it was a worthwhile cost to pay. Agree or disagree, that's at least honest."

"But everyone knows why we lost. And any honest conversation about where we go from here must start here," the vice president said, while saying he remains hopeful "about our country."

SpaceX, Tesla stocks slide as Democrats sweep midterms

Shares of SpaceX and Tesla, companies run by tech billionaire and world's richest man Elon Musk, were both down about 5%, as US markets opened on Wednesday.

Musk is a Republican megadonor and had spent nearly $500 million to back Republican candidates in the midterms, including efforts to keep Republicans in power in Texas, a state home to both the automaker Tesla and rocket-to-AI company SpaceX, as well as being a hub to AI data centers.

But with Democrats seizing both houses of Congress and Gina Hinojosa defeating Greg Abbott in the Texas governor's race, Musk and his companies could face increasing scrutiny by Congress as well as Texas state authorities.
