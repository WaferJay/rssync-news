July sees sharp jump in German factory orders

German factory orders climbed in July for the third month in a row, according to official data from federal statistics office Destatis released on Friday,

Month-on-month industrial orders rose 2.5%, beating one expectation of just a 0.5% increase in a poll of analysts by the financial data platform FactSet.

The increase was mainly driven by an "exceptionally high volume" of large-scale orders for ships, railway rolling stock and aircraft, according to a Destatis press release. 

Industrial orders serve as an indicator of future business activity, and the recent increase is being interpreted by some analysts as a sign Germany's struggling economy is starting to recover. 

This week, the Ifo and RWI economic research institutes both sharply revised their 2026 GDP growth forecasts higher by 1.3 to 1.4%

"It has been a long while since such we have had such positive headlines about the German economy," LBBW bank analyst Jens-Oliver Niklasch told AFP news agency.

Factors including high energy prices, the rise of Chinese exports in competitive markets, and a faltering automotive sector have all weighed heavily on Germany's growth in recent years. 

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has vowed to borrow and spend hundreds of billions of euros in the coming years to boost industrial growth, mainly through defense and infrastructure.

Hybrid attacks 'no coincidence' ahead of Sunday's state election — Interior Ministry

A German Interior Ministry spokesperson said it is "no coincidence" that several suspected acts of sabotage on electrical infrastructure have taken place this week, just days ahead of an important state election in Saxony-Anhalt. 

"In the context of the ‌upcoming elections ... there is ‌an expectation that this may increase," the spokesperson said at a regular news conference.

Polling shows the election in the eastern German state on Sunday could see the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) come out on top, and potentially be able to govern a German state for the first timewith an outright majority. 

Police are investigating at least three suspected power grid sabotage attempts this week; two in North Rhine-Westphalia, and one in Brandenburg. Officials have not yet named suspects or a motive in any of the incidents.

Open electrical cabinet sparks police operation at Lower Saxony substation

Early Friday morning, a police patrol spotted an open cabinet at a power substation in the village of Ganderkesee, near Bremen in the northwestern state of Lower Saxony.

The find comes amid three suspected acts of sabotage at different substations in Germany over the past week. 

The officer who spotted the open cabinet called in additional personnel, a police spokesperson said Friday.

"Due to the special significance of the technical facility, the premises were immediately and thoroughly searched," the spokeswoman wrote.

No damage or unauthorized persons were found at the location.

"At no time was there any danger to the public," the spokesperson said. 

Meanwhile, police near the western German city of Aachen are searching an area near a power plant in Weisweiler after receiving a letter from a man claiming responsibility for attempted sabotages elsewhere in Germany this week, and naming Weisweiler as a potential target.

Police have not said whether the man's claims are credible, nor whether any suspicious objects have been found during Friday morning's search.

Suspects in Munich arson attack to appear in court

The Bavarian State Criminal Police Office (LKA) on Friday said a 28-year-old woman and a 38-year-old man detained in connection with an arson attack in Munich are due to appear in court where a judge will decide whether to issue a formal arrest order.

Early Thursday morning, police were called to a fire at a construction site located near buildings in Munich's Berg am Laim neighborhood belonging to defense tech companies, including Helsing and Rohde & Schwarz.

Police said incendiary devices were thrown from a car. One was extinguished and did not cause any damage. Another was defused.

The suspects, both Bulgarian citizens, were arrested shortly after the incident at a nearby gas station. Police have not released any information about a possible motive.

Volkswagen shares jump after 100,000 job cuts announced

Markets on Friday responded positively after German automobile giant Volkswagen announced a major restructure that averted an escalation with unions and shareholder ​Lower ‌Saxony.

The carmaker announced on Thursday that it would be cutting a total of 100,000 jobs by the end of the decade in what is set to be the biggest restructuring ever seen in the global car industry.

The company said that management and trade unions had approved a plan involving the reduction of a further 50,000 jobs, in addition to 50,000 redundancies already agreed.

"It is essential to systematically align workforce levels with economic realities," read a statement from the multi-brand Volkswagen group which, apart from VW itself, also includes Audi and Porsche.

The total of 100,000 cuts amount to about 15% of Volkswagen staff worldwide. The cull eclipses the 50,000 job cuts General Motors made after it declared bankruptcy in 2009.

Unions and management had publicly rowed before signing off on the plans, with the former accusing the latter of not being honest with the workforce after the figure of 100,000 possible job cuts surfaced in the media before being communicated internally.

Volkswagen also said the long-term future of four major German plants – in Hannover, Emden, Zwickau and Neckarsulm — could not be guaranteed.

Their closure would mark the first full-scale shuttering of Volkswagen factories in its home country.

Parties in Saxony-Anhalt hold final campaign events ahead of Sunday's polls

The eastern German state of Saxony-Anhalt goes to the polls on Sunday, and several parties are holding closing rallies on Friday to drum up support among undecided voters.

According to a survey by polling group "Forschungsgruppe Wahlen" cited by DPA news agency, 31% of respondents are still undecided.

Polls are showing the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party in the lead as it aims for an a absolute majority that would allow it to lead a state government in Germany for the first time. 

The AfD's lead candidate Ulrich Siegmund and national party chairman Tino Chrupalla are due at a campaign event in the state capital, Magdeburg.

Sven Schulze, currently state premierand lead candidate for the conservative Christian Democratic Union (CDU), also has engagements in Magdeburg planned for Friday.

The Left Party's lead candidate Eva von Angern is appearing in Magdeburg, and the city of Halle, alongside leading party figures Heidi Reichinnek, Gregor Gysi and Dietmar Bartsch.

The Greens are planning events in Halle and Naumburg, with lead candidate Susan Sziborra-Seidlitz joined by federal party chairman Felix Banaszak.

Saxony-Anhalt's current coalition government, comprising the CDU, center-left Social Democratic Party (SPD) and the pro-business Free Democrats (FDP),  is set to lose its majority, according to polls.

Police suspect another sabotage attempt near Dormagen substation

Police in western Germany say a suspicious device found at an electricity substation in the small city of Dormagen between Cologne and Düsseldorf was likely intended to "cause damage."

"Someone apparently tried to deliberately cause a short circuit," a Cologne police spokesperson told AFP news agency. 

The object was discovered late Thursday by a passer-by. A police spokesperson said it may have been a "suspicious launching device." 

The Bild tabloid reported that homemade rockets with launching devices, including timers and cooper wires, were discovered. Police have not confirmed the report. 

The new discovery Friday is the latest this week in what police say are attempts at damaging critical infrastructure. 

The first incident on Tuesday near the Jänschwalde coal power plant in the eastern state of Brandenburg involved homemade projectiles resembling New Year's Eve fireworks that can short out high-voltage power lines by shooting a grounding wire over them.

A day later, similar objects were found near a power station in Bergheim, in the western state of North Rhine-Westphalia.
