Rights court rules Turkey must release jailed lawmaker Atalay

Sertac Comak with dpa, AFP
11/17/2026November 17, 2026

The European Court of Human Rights has called for the release of imprisoned elected lawmaker Can Atalay. Atalay has been in prison for almost five years despite rulings from the Turkish Constitutional Court.

The European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) ruled against Turkey over the detention of human rights lawyer and elected lawmaker Can Atalay.

The ECHR published its decision on Tuesday, saying that Turkey must release Atalay and allow him to take up his seat in the Turkish parliament.

The court ruled that his continued detention had been intended to silence him and undermine political pluralism.

It ordered Turkey to pay him €32,500 (about $35,700) in compensation.

The judges stressed that Atalay's case should not be considered separately, but rather in the wider context of the detention of political opponents, human rights activists and journalists in Turkey.

No future in Turkey? Growing exodus of young Turks

Turkey was among the original signatories of the European Convention on Human Rights. Yet it has previously refused to comply with the court's decisions in the cases of rights activist Osman Kavala and prominent Kurdish politician Selahattin Demirtas.

From leading activist to a jailed member of parliament

As a lawyer, Atalay represented victims in numerous social and political cases in Turkey, as well as in freedom of speech cases involving journalists and writers.

He was also among the leading voices in the 2013 Gezi protests, which the government called an attempted coup.

Because of his involvement in the protests, Atalay, along with seven others, was sentenced to 18 years in prison in April 2022 for "aiding and abetting an attempted coup."

Continuing his struggle from the infamous Silivri prison, Atalay ran as a candidate for the Workers' Party of Turkey (TIP) in the 2023 parliamentary elections.

A candidate for the earthquake-hit province of Hatay, he won his seat but was denied release despite multiple rulings by the Constitutional Court, Turkey's top court.

His parliamentary status was revoked in 2024, a decision the Constitutional Court later overturned. President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's party stopped the opposition from formally reading the decision in parliament.

Although a year later an opposition lawmaker read the decision in parliament, Atalay remains in jail.

After the ECHR's ruling was published, TIP leader Erkan Bas took to social media to call for Atalay's release and the restoration of his parliamentary mandate.
