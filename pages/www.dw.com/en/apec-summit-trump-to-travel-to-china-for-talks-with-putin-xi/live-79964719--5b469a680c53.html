Canada, China taking steps to widen access for financial firms, says Carney

Canada and China are moving to give each other's financial firms greater access to their markets, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said during a speech at the APEC summit. 

"When our businesses depend on ​too few currencies or single payment channels, disruption, when it comes, can spread quickly," ​he said, adding that China and Canada had renewed the ​currency swap arrangement ‌between their central banks and were issuing each other's currencies.

Carney's comments came a day after Carney and President Xi held talks in Shenzhen to produce deals to further reduce tariffs. Ottawa is re-engaging with China after years of strained relations as it looks to reduce its reliance on the US.

Xi calls for 'free and open trade' as counter to US tariffs

President Xi pledged to stand against trade protectionism in his speech at the APEC summit on Wednesday, in a subtle slight towards the United States and the trade war waged by its president. 

Xi urged support for what he called "free and open trade," trying to ​portray his country as a stable partner on the global stage in contrast with its main economic rival, the US, which has raised tariffs ‌under President Trump.

His remarks came after Washington announced further restrictions on China's access to advanced AI chips from companies such as Nvidia, further complicating a trade truce between the two superpowers.

Xi urged that China and Russia, together, take concrete measures to further cooperation and "lead and advance the ⁠trend toward ​a multipolar world."

Chinese and Russian news agencies reported at around noon on Wednesday that Xi and Putin had met for talks. That's the fifth time the two leaders have met this year, including once by video link.

China's Xi calls for 'free and open' trade, deeper AI cooperation at APEC opening

Karl Sexton

China's President Xi Jinping on Wednesday called for more international cooperation on AI and "free and open" trade.

Xi made the remarks as he opened a meeting of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) in Shenzhen, southern China, where he is expected to meet US President Donald Trump and Russia's Vladimir Putin, who landed overnight, on Thursday. 

Trump is due to arrive in Shenzhen later on Wednesday.

"We must ​firmly uphold the rules-based multilateral trading system with the World Trade Organization at its core, support free ​and open trade and investment, maintain the stability and smooth operation of ​global ‌industrial and supply chains," Xi said.

"We should actively build partnerships for scientific and technological innovation, deepen cooperation in the digital economy and artificial intelligence and promote a green and low-carbon transition," the Chinese leader said.

Trade tensions have loomed over the world's two largest economies as the summit neared.

Trump's wide-ranging tariffs have disrupted global trade.

China has responded with its own import levies and has threatened to withhold crucial exports.

Skip next section Trump in Asia: What happened Tuesday and what is expected on Wednesday
11/17/2026November 17, 2026

Trump in Asia: What happened Tuesday and what is expected on Wednesday

Wesley Dockery

Trump landed in the Philippines on Tuesday to meet with the rest of the Quad (Australia, Japan, India) and Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) leaders as he embarked on another Asia tour of his second term. 

The visit was met with protests against the Trump administration's Pax Silica initiative and the war with Iran, which has driven up global energy prices. 

Upon his arrival, Trump waved small US and Filipino flags before speaking to the Quad Leaders at the Solaire Resort.

During the talks, Trump vowed to be there for the Quad and praised Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Japan's Sanae Takaichi as "tough." He announced that the Quad nations are working on an accelerator to speed up AI development. 

Trump then attended an ASEAN summit dinner where he was the "guest of honor." During his talks with ASEAN leaders, he met with Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul. Trump and Anutin signed a statement paving the way for a US-Thai trade deal.

What will happen on Wednesday?

On Wednesday, Trump will depart the Philippines and fly to Shenzhen, China, where he will attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Leader's (APEC) meeting.

Asian leaders such as Chinese President Xi Jinping and South Korean President Lee Jae Myung will participate, along with Russian President Vladimir Putin, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum, among others. 

On Thursday, Trump is scheduled to hold a trilateral meeting with Xi and Putin, with the Russian invasion of Ukraine possibly discussed, along with Iran and denuclearization.

Trump signs statement on trade deal with Thailand

Wesley Dockery

During his visit to Manila, Trump signed a statement regarding an upcoming trade deal with Thailand. 

"Thailand will eliminate all tariffs on US exports, which is something that we've been after for a long time," Trump said alongside Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul. He added that Thailand will also purchase "an estimated $2.6 billion (almost €2.7 billion) in additional US agricultural products." 

"This is an incredible victory... I look forward to finalizing the details of our formal agreement in the coming weeks," the president added.   

Anutin said: "I believe the agreement will be greater dynamics to our trade relations and open new opportunities to all stakeholders."  

"Our partnership is beyond trade and investment," he added. 

The trade deal with Thailand will be a major relief for the Thai economy. The US serves as Thailand's biggest export market. 

In 2025, Trump had imposed tariffs of 36% on Thai goods coming into the US, before lowering the levy to 19%.
